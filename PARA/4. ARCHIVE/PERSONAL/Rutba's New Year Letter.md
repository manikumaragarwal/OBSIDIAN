Dear Rutba,

I hope this year you make so many more friends, because more people deserve to experience what it's like to know someone with a heart like yours.

I know we had the worst possible start. I literally yelled "Fuck off" at you thinking you were some random guy, and then I realized... oh god, that's the quiet girl from class. I still cringe thinking about it. But I'm really glad you gave me a the option of having an first impression ever after that.

These past few weeks have been weird for me in the best way. You showed me something I didn't really understand before, that kindness isn't weak. It's actually the hardest thing to do, and you make it look so easy.

I keep replaying that day at Connaught Place. We were all hanging out, having a good time, and I was just in my own head. But you noticed Aalap was struggling. You saw his OCD was making things hard and you actually cared enough to do something about it. I don't know if you realize how rare that is. Most people, including me sometimes, are too caught up in themselves to notice when someone's drowning right next to them.

You inspire me to be better. I want to be the person who makes sure no one's standing alone in a corner. I want to be someone people can come to when everything feels like shit. I know I probably sound like some naive, delusional kid right now, but honestly? I'm happy being that kid. I wish more people would let themselves be that hopeful.

Because deep down, I just don't want anyone to feel the way I did growing up. I don't want someone to be the punching bag. I don't want someone to be the person everyone redirects the joke to when they're getting roasted. I don't want anyone to always be the last choice, the backup friend.

When you told me I was your friend, something in me just... relaxed. Like a part of me I didn't even know was tense finally let go. Growing up, I didn't have many friends. I was that kid people made fun of. The verbal punching bag. So when you said it, when you actually seemed happy about it, that meant everything.

You also make me believe I can actually do something with my life. I don't know how you do that, but you do.

And thank god you're not into all the gossip drama. My old friends were always talking shit about people behind their backs, and I'd join in because it was fun, but I'd feel guilty the whole time. You just... don't do that. And somehow being around you is changing me without you even trying.

You're almost too kind, you know? You're literally the only girl I know who says "aap" instead of "tum." You say thank you for the smallest things. And when I'm with you, being nice doesn't feel like work. It just feels normal. We've got this stupid thing now where we keep thanking each other and then telling each other to stop saying thank you. It's ridiculous.

Also, you feed me. Yakhni, sandwiches, Kashmiri food I'd never even heard of before. You have no idea how much I appreciate that.

I saw what happened with the class monitor thing. People dumped everything on you, got mad at you for no reason, wouldn't let you study even when exams were close. And that asshole who made you cry? I'm still angry about that. You didn't deserve any of it. But watching you handle all that with so much grace... I don't think I could've done that.

I know you miss your Nani, I know you miss your Home. I know family is everything to you.

And I know you sometimes feel like you don't fit in here because you're from Kashmir and Delhi feels different. But Rutba, listen to me. You don't need to change anything about yourself to belong here or anywhere. Being exactly who you are is what makes you special. The people who matter will see that. You're not some outsider trying to fit into a mold. You're someone who makes every space better just by being in it.

We've only known each other for three-four weeks. Four weeks since late November, and now it's already New Year. That's insane to me. Because you've become so important to me in such a short time. I guess that's what you taught me, that time doesn't really matter when it comes to real friendship.

Thank you for being my friend. Thank you for being exactly who you are.

Happy New Year, Rutba. I hope this year brings you back to your Family more often, brings you closer to whatever dreams you're holding onto, and reminds you every day that you belong exactly where you are.

Manish
