Hi,

I’ve been thinking about how years end.  
Not loudly, not with fireworks in the head, but quietly.  
Like dust settling after a long day.

This year made me look back more than I expected.  
At people. At silences. At things that were once so natural they didn’t need naming.

You came up, of course.  
Not in a dramatic way. Just… gently.  
Like something familiar I hadn’t visited in a long time.

There was a version of my life where you were just there.  
Studying, talking, pushing each other forward, laughing at nonsense.  
It wasn’t something I questioned. It felt steady. Safe.  
And I think I understood that value only after it loosened its grip on my days.

Somewhere along the way, things became complicated.  
Feelings got tangled. Timing stopped cooperating.  
And instead of knowing how to sit with that honestly, I chose distance.  
Not because I didn’t care, but because I didn’t know how to hold everything at once.

I don’t want to reopen anything.  
I don’t want explanations or reassurances or long conversations about what went wrong.  
This isn’t that kind of letter.

It’s just me saying that I’m grateful.  
For what we were. For what we shared.  
For the version of myself that existed more easily because you were around.

The new year feels like a clean pause.  
Not a reset button, just a breath.  
And before moving forward, I wanted to acknowledge the past without bitterness or regret.

We’ve both grown into different lives now.  
Different rhythms, different priorities.  
And that’s okay. That’s how it’s supposed to be.

I genuinely hope you keep doing well.  
That you keep building the life you want, with the same quiet determination I always admired.  
And that whatever comes next for you feels earned and light.

You don’t have to reply to this.  
There’s no expectation tied to these words.  
I just didn’t want to carry them unspoken into another year.

Some friendships don’t stay the same forever,  
but that doesn’t make them any less real.

I wish you a good year ahead.  
A steady one. A kind one.

Take care,  
Manish
