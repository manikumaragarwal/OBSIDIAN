# 1. AGE BACKGROUND (2–3 MARKS / INTRODUCTION)

### 18th Century = Neoclassical / Augustan Age

**Keywords**

- Reason over emotion
    
- Wit and satire
    
- Order, balance, decorum
    
- Classical influence (Horace, Virgil)
    
- Rise of middle class
    
- Coffee-house culture
    
- Journalism and periodicals
    
- Growth of empire and colonial wealth
    

### Important Features

- Age of Prose and Reason
    
- Heroic Couplet dominant
    
- Satire = social correction
    
- Mock-Epic becomes popular
    

---

# 2. ALEXANDER POPE

### Remember

- Greatest poet of Augustan Age
    
- Master of **Heroic Couplet**
    
    - iambic pentameter
        
    - rhyming couplets
        

### Life Influence

- Physically deformed due to spinal tuberculosis
    
- Social outsider
    
- Developed sharp wit and satire
    
- Critical of vanity, artificiality and social pretensions
    

### Other Works

- _The Dunciad_
    
- _Essay on Criticism_
    
- _Essay on Man_
    

---

# 3. ORIGIN OF THE POEM

### Real Incident

Lord Petre cut a lock of hair from Arabella Fermor.

↓

Families quarrelled.

↓

John Caryll asked Pope to reconcile them through humour.

↓

Result = _The Rape of the Lock_

---

# 4. MOCK EPIC (MOST IMPORTANT)

### Definition

A mock epic uses:

- Epic style
    
- Epic language
    
- Epic machinery
    

for

- Trivial subject matter
    

### Pope's Formula

**Trojan War**  
→ replaced by

**Stolen lock of hair**

### Epic Elements Used

- Invocation
    
- Supernatural machinery
    
- Prophecy
    
- Epic battles
    
- Arming scenes
    
- Journey to underworld
    
- Divine intervention
    
- Transformation into constellation
    

### Purpose

- Satirize aristocratic society
    
- Expose vanity and triviality
    
- Make society laugh at itself
    

---

# 5. CENTRAL THEMES

## Vanity

Belinda worships beauty.

Mirror becomes idol.

Toilette becomes religious ritual.

Beauty > morality.

---

## Appearance vs Reality

Cross worn as ornament.

Religion reduced to fashion.

Honor becomes social reputation.

Virtue becomes public image.

---

## Artificiality

Society values:

- cosmetics
    
- fashion
    
- gossip
    
- flirtation
    

more than:

- intelligence
    
- morality
    
- genuine emotion
    

---

## Female Beauty as Power

Belinda's locks:

- traps
    
- chains
    
- snares
    

Beauty functions like a weapon.

---

## Trivialization of Values

Examples:

- Chastity = broken jar
    
- Honor = stained dress
    
- Husband = lapdog
    

Everything placed on same level.

---

## Satire of Aristocratic Society

Pope attacks:

- idleness
    
- vanity
    
- flirtation
    
- gossip
    
- obsession with appearance
    

---

# 8. CANTO-WISE MEMORY MAP

## CANTO I

### Key Events

- Invocation
    
- Belinda sleeping
    
- Ariel's warning
    
- Prophecy
    
- Toilette scene
    

### Critical Points

- Parody of epic invocation
    
- Parody of Satan tempting Eve
    
- Toilette = religious ceremony
    
- "Puffs, Powders, Patches, Bibles, Billet-doux"
    

### Exam Line

**Belinda worships her own reflection.**

---

## CANTO II

### Key Events

- Thames journey
    
- Description of lock
    
- Baron's vow
    
- Ariel organizes sylphs
    

### Critical Points

- Lock becomes epic object
    
- Baron behaves like epic hero
    
- Cross reduced to ornament
    
- Epic shield parody = petticoat
    

### Exam Line

**Beauty is transformed into a military weapon.**

---

## CANTO III

### Key Events

- Hampton Court
    
- Ombre game
    
- Coffee ritual
    
- Cutting of lock
    

### Critical Points

- Ombre = mock epic battlefield
    
- Coffee = epic banquet
    
- Scissors = fatal engine
    
- Ariel abandons Belinda
    

### Exam Line

**The epic battle culminates not in death but in a haircut.**

---

## CANTO IV

### Key Events

- Belinda's despair
    
- Cave of Spleen
    
- Umbriel's mission
    
- Sir Plume
    
- Belinda's lament
    

### Critical Points

- Underworld parody
    
- Female hysteria satirized
    
- Society values appearance over virtue
    

### MOST IMPORTANT

"Hairs less in sight"

Meaning:

Belinda values public beauty more than actual chastity.

This is Pope's sharpest satire.

---

## CANTO V

### Key Events

- Clarissa's speech
    
- Drawing-room battle
    
- Jove's scales
    
- Snuff attack
    
- Lock becomes star
    

### Critical Points

### Clarissa = Voice of Reason

Most important moral:

**"Charms strike the sight, but merit wins the soul."**

### Ending

Lock becomes constellation.

Mock-epic version of heroic immortality.

# 10. IMPORTANT DEVICES

## Heroic Couplet

- Rhyming couplets
    
- iambic pentameter
    

Most important stylistic feature.

---

## Zeugma

One word governs serious + trivial objects.

Examples:

- stain honor / stain dress
    
- lose heart / lose necklace
    
- break Diana's law / break china jar
    

### Function

Shows distorted priorities.

---

## Juxtaposition

Grand language + trivial events.

Core technique of mock epic.
