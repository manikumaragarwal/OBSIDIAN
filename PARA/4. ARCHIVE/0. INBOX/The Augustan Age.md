For your one-month prep, data tables are excellent for "mapping" the complex themes of the 18th century into digestible chunks. In the **Audio Overview (Deep Dive)** mode of NotebookLM, the AI will use your sources to synthesize information, but it won't "display" a table for you to look at—it will talk about it.

However, you can ask the **NotebookLM Chat** (where we are now) to generate these tables for you to copy into your Anki cards or study notes.

Here are three types of data tables you should generate, along with what information goes in each:

### 1. The "Comparative Satire" Table

This is the most important for your exam. It helps you distinguish between the different _types_ of satire used by Pope, Swift, and Goldsmith.

| **Column Name**       | **What to Fill Here**                                                                        |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Author/Text**       | The specific work (e.g., _Gulliver's Travels_).                                              |
| **Target of Satire**  | Who or what is being mocked? (e.g., Political corruption, human nature).                     |
| **Satirical Mode**    | Is it **Horatian** (gentle, playful like Pope) or **Juvenalian** (harsh, biting like Swift)? |
| **Key Example/Quote** | A specific scene from your sources (e.g., The "Tightrope dancing" in Lilliput).              |

### 2. The "Symbolism & Allegory" Table

Use this to keep track of the "supernatural machinery" and the various lands Gulliver visits.

| **Column Name**      | **What to Fill Here**                                                                                |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Symbol/Object**    | The item or character (e.g., Belinda’s Lock, The Laputa Floating Island).                            |
| **Literal Meaning**  | What is it in the story? (e.g., A piece of hair, a scientific academy).                              |
| **Symbolic Meaning** | What does it represent in 18th-century society? (e.g., Female vanity, detached theoretical science). |
| **Exam relevance**   | Is it a short note topic in the **PREVIOUS YEAR QUESTIONS**?                                         |

### 3. The "DU Exam Pattern" Table

Fill this by extracting data from your **"PREVIOUS YEAR QUESTIONS"** file to spot trends.

|**Column Name**|**What to Fill Here**|
|---|---|
|**Unit/Text**|Unit 1 (Pope), Unit 2 (Swift), etc.|
|**Frequent 10-Mark Topics**|Recurring short note themes (e.g., "Clarissa's speech", "Yahoos").|
|**Frequent 20-Mark Topics**|Recurring essay questions (e.g., "Mock-epic form", "Gulliver as an unreliable narrator").|
|**Confidence Level**|Rate your readiness for this specific topic (1–5).|
