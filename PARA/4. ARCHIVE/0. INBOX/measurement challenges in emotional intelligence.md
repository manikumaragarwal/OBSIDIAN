# measurement challenges in emotional intelligence

## The Problem
Measuring emotional intelligence is complex and traditional methods have significant limitations.

## Three Approaches

### 1. Self-Report (Asking the Person)
- **Limitation**: Self-knowledge is limited
- **Example**: 80% of Yale students say they're more emotionally intelligent than their neighbor (statistical impossibility)
- **Reference point problem**: "Compared to my father I am an emotional genius; compared to the Dalai Lama I need some work"

### 2. Informant/360 Reports (Asking Others)
- **Limitation**: Measures reputation, not skills
- **Problem**: Others' ratings are influenced by whether they like you
- Not necessarily about emotional intelligence ability

### 3. Ability-Based Assessment (Solving Problems)
- **Preferred method**: Actually measuring skills
- **Original tool**: MSCEIT (Mayer-Salovey-Caruso Emotional Intelligence Test)
- **New approach**: Virtual tests using avatars and gaming
- **Method**: Present emotion-related scenarios, measure responses

## The Research Reality
- Self-report measures of EI have no validity
- Simple questionnaires asking "how good are you at regulating emotions" don't work
- Need actual behavioral or ability-based measures

## The Future
Yale's Center is working with gaming companies to build:
- Virtual reality emotional intelligence tests
- Avatar-based scenarios
- Real-time assessment of emotional skills
- More engaging and valid measurement tools
