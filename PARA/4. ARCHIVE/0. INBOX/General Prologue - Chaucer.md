## Class Notes: Geoffrey Chaucer (c. 1340–1400)

### I. Biographical Context

- **The "Father of English Literature":** Earned this title by choosing to write in **English** at a time when French and Latin were the dominant languages for high art and scholarship.
- **Social Background:** Born to London wine merchants; his career as a page for the Countess of Ulster introduced him to courtly culture and romantic literature.
- **Professional Versatility:** Served three kings in various roles:
    - Diplomat
    - Comptroller of Customs
    - Justice of the Peace
- **The Italian Influence:** A 1372 diplomatic trip to Italy is considered a major turning point that shifted his literary style and perspective.
    
### II. Literary Evolution: The Four Phases

Chaucer’s work progressed from imitation and translation to the creation of unique English masterpieces.

#### A. Early Works (The French Influence)
- **Focus:** Heavily modeled on French poetic ideals.
- **Key Works:**
    - _Roman de la Rose_ (Translation)
    - _The Book of the Duchess_ (His first major original work; an elegy for Queen Blanche).
    - Shorter poems: _Pity_, _Complaint to his Lady_, and _A.B.C._
#### B. Middle Works & Narrative Verse
- **Focus:** Increasing confidence in English; introduction of irony and humor.
- **Key Works:**
    - _Life of S. Cecilia_, _Stories of Griselda and Constance_
    - _De Consolatione Philosophiae_ (Translation of Boethius).
    - **Marriage Poems:** _The House of Fame_ and _The Parliament of Fowles_.
#### C. Major Masterpieces (The 1380s)
- **_Troilus and Criseyde_**: Considered one of his greatest individual works.
- **_The Legend of Good Women_**: An attempt at a linked collection of stories (abandoned due to "monotonous" themes).
- **_The Canterbury Tales_** (begun c. 1387):
    - His **magnum opus**.
    - **Structure:** Uses a pilgrimage as a "frame story" to link various tales.
    - **Styles:** Includes chivalric romance, allegory, beast epics, and mock sermons.
#### D. Late/Minor Poems
- **Key Works:** _The Former Age_, _Fortune_, _Truth_, and _Gentilesse_.
- **Final Work:** _Compleynt of Chaucer to his Purse_.

### III. Poetic Vision & Legacy
- **Realism over Romance:** Instead of focusing on fading knightly legends, he portrayed **everyday English people** from all social classes.
- **Technical Innovation:**
    - Introduced the **seven-line stanza**.
    - Utilized **10- or 11-syllable couplets** to create a more natural, "conversational" flow.
- **Tone and Style:**
    - Replaced rigid, older structures with a "cheerfully hopeful" worldview.
    - Famous for **subtle irony**, **genial tolerance** for human flaws, and vivid **character portraits**.
> [!NOTE]
> **The Core Concept:** Chaucer transformed English from a "common" dialect into a legitimate literary medium. He shifted the focus of poetry from stylized, borrowed European tropes to **authentic human realism**. By blending the sophisticated structures of Italian and French poetry with the grit and humor of daily English life, he established the foundation for modern English narrative style.

### IV. 14th-Century England: An Era of Transition

The century was a bridge between the **Medieval** and the **Modern** world, characterized by the decline of feudalism and the rise of capitalism.

#### A. National Identity and Language

- **Nationalism:** Early military victories against France and Scotland sparked a sense of English pride.
    
- **Linguistic Shift:** English began replacing French and Latin in law and literature, a movement led by Chaucer’s work.
    

#### B. The Impact of the Black Death (1348)

- **Demographics:** The plague wiped out nearly **50% of the population**.
    
- **Labor Crisis:** The massive loss of life created a labor shortage, giving peasants newfound bargaining power.
    
- **The Peasant's Revolt (1381):** A violent uprising triggered by the Crown's attempt to suppress rising wage demands.
    

#### C. The New Social Hierarchy

- **The Middle Class:** Trade and commerce created a "nouveau riche" class that broke down rigid feudal barriers.
    
- **Social Mobility:** Wealth allowed for movement between classes, often expressed through extravagant displays of food and fashion.
    

#### D. Institutional Corruption & Education

- **Church Corruption:** The Church became deeply materialistic. Figures like "begging friars" and "pardoners" were notorious for greed and extorting the poor.
    
- **Spread of Literacy:** Education expanded beyond the clergy via new college systems and the Inns of Court.
    
- **The "Pre-Printing" Era:** Because books were still hand-copied, they remained rare and high-value status symbols.
    

---

### Concept Summary: The "Chaucerian Synthesis"

> **The Core Concept:** Chaucer’s genius lay in his ability to mirror a **society in flux**. His poetry captured the tension of the 14th century: the shift from the old feudal/religious order to a new, individualistic middle-class reality. By using the "vulgar" English language to describe "common" people, he effectively democratized literature and laid the groundwork for the modern English identity.
