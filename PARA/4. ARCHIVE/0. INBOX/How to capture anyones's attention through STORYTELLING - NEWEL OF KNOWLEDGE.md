### CRYSTAL BALL

### BACKPACK TECHNIQUE

- **put a backpack on the audience**
  In Ocean eleven, the heist is always fully outlined... this ensures the audience knows exactly what the storyteller is aiming at and will feel the same level of frustration and angst when it's not achieved.

### THE HOURGLASS TECHNIQUE

- Add more details to disguise the moment you're going to reveal later in the story...
- mention seemingly insignificant details to create suspense. (it's like using [[PARADOX OF CHOICE]] to confuse the audience on what [[CHEKOV'S GUN|Chekov's GUN]] (or [[FORESHADOWING|foreshadowing]]) to choose from..)
  - e.g: in [[SHUTTER ISLAND]], we never noticed that the glass disappeared from the nurses hands... because we were confused by use of the hourglass techniques...

## 5. HOW TO END A STORY

### THE BEST STORIES HAVE MESSY ENDING?

- end story with **Heart** not with pun, joke or play on words..
- we listen to story to be moved... so be **VULNERABLE** at the ending.

- to make stories moving... end in a place that is moving, vulnerable and revealing.. then establish connection.

## 6. HUMOR & WHEN TO USE IT

> "Comedians want to be funny. Storytellers want to be remembered"

### MILKCANS & BASEBALLS | BABIES & BLENDERS

- Milk cans and baseballs (building suspense though including seemingly insignificant details to then knock them down with humour)
- Babies and blenders (including two things together which shouldn’t be together) “the kid was waving his pencil around like an ak-47”

- Humour is optional. Stakes are nonnegotiable.
- The longer you speak = the more articulate and captivating
  you have to be. So talk less. Make brevity your gift.

### The negative description trumps the positive:

“’m dumb, ugly and annoying.” |s worse than “I’m not smart, I’m not good looking, I’m not kind”. Because the latter contains a BUT - it entertains both possibilities

- Bad: | was lost. Heather was my ex girlfriend. | was penniless.
- Good: | couldn’t find my way home. Heather was no longer my girlfriend. | didn’t have a lot of money.

> “_You own everything that happened to you. Tell your stories. If people wanted you to write warmly about them, they should have behaved better._”

## 8. CARDINAL SINS OF STORYTELLING (DON'T DO THIS)

- Don’t tell someone else’s story (If you are going to tell someone else’s story, tell it from your perspective)

- Be careful of ambulance stories (stories about near death experiences etc. are likely to spark “wow!” In the audience but are unlikely to spark relatability in the audience. Telling a story about an everyday aspect of life we all experience (like relationship issues etc.) is likely to be more relatable)

- ==Don’t interrupt your story with narration. “The thing you have to understand about my life at this point is...”. You never see movies do this. Instead, include description WITHIN the story.==

## SUMMARY:

- Structure = the order of events.
- Backpack = what audience wants.
- Crystal Ball = what audience predicts.
- Hourglass = what audience notices.
- Stakes = what audience fears.
