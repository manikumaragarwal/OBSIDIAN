# UNIT 1 :Foundation and Framework
## 1. What is Emotional Intelligence?

- **Definition:** Emotional intelligence is the ability to understand and manage your own emotions, as well as recognize and influence the emotions of those around you.
    
- **Origins & Popularization:**
    
    - The term was first coined in 1990 by researchers **John Mayer and Peter Salovey**.
        
    - It was later popularized by psychologist **Daniel Goleman**.
        
- **Core Purpose:** Psychologists (including Salovey and Mayer) have developed models that treat emotional intelligence as a crucial set of skills needed to navigate the complexities of human interactions.
    

## 2. 4 Core Competencies of Emotional Intelligence

_(According to the Harvard Business School Online framework, structured as an interaction between Awareness/Actions and Self/Others)_

- **Self-Awareness:** Focuses on the _Self_ and _Awareness_. Recognizing your own internal emotional states.
    
- **Social Awareness:** Focuses on _Others_ and _Awareness_. Recognizing and reading the emotions of people around you.
    
- **Self-Management:** Focuses on the _Self_ and _Actions_. Taking steps to regulate, control, and manage your own emotions.
    
- **Relationship Management:** Focuses on _Others_ and _Actions_. Utilizing emotional insights to handle, influence, and guide your interactions with others effectively.
    

## 3. Benefits of Emotional Intelligence

- **Improved Relationships:** Enhances interpersonal skills, fostering healthier and more positive connections with others.
    
- **Effective Communication:** Individuals with high EI are better at expressing themselves and understanding others, which leads to clearer and more empathetic communication.
    
- **Conflict Resolution:** Helps in managing and resolving conflicts by promoting understanding, compromise, and finding common ground.
    
- **Stress Management:** People with strong EI are generally better equipped to cope with stress because they can regulate their own emotions and adapt to challenging situations.
    
- **Enhanced Leadership:** A key trait in effective leaders, enabling them to inspire and motivate others, build trust, and navigate complex team dynamics.
    

## 4. Different Models of Emotional Intelligence

### Model I: Ability Model (Peter Salovey & John Mayer)

This model structures Emotional Intelligence as a set of four distinct cognitive-emotional abilities:

1. **Perceiving Emotions:** Recognizing and accurately perceiving emotions in oneself and others. This includes sensitivity to facial expressions, body language, and other nonverbal cues.
    
2. **Using Emotions:** The capacity to harness emotions to facilitate various cognitive activities, such as thinking and problem-solving. This involves channeling emotions to enhance creativity and decision-making.
    
3. **Understanding Emotions:** Comprehending the complex relationships between emotions and being able to understand emotional transitions. It requires grasping the direct impact of emotions on thoughts, behaviors, and relationships.
    
4. **Managing Emotions:** The ability to regulate and manage one's own emotions effectively, while also handling and influencing the emotions of others to foster positive interpersonal relationships.
    

### Model II: Mixed Model (Daniel Goleman)

Goleman’s model combines emotional abilities with personality traits, broken down into five key components:

- **Self-awareness:** Recognizing and understanding one's own emotions, including their direct impact on thoughts and behavior.
    
- **Self-regulation:** Managing and controlling one's emotions, impulses, and reactions—especially when facing challenging situations.
    
- **Motivation:** Being internally driven to achieve goals, persisting in the face of setbacks, and maintaining a positive attitude.
    
- **Empathy:** Understanding and sensing the emotions of others, and responding to them with genuine compassion.
    
- **Social skills:** Effectively navigating social situations, communicating clearly with others, and building/maintaining strong relationships.
    

### Model III: Trait Model (Konstantin Vasily Petrides)

Also known as the Trait Emotional Intelligence (Trait EI) model, this framework views EI as a collection of personality facets and self-perceptions:

- **Emotional Self-awareness (ESA):** Recognizing and understanding one's own emotions.
    
- **Emotional Expression (EE):** Effectively expressing one's emotions to other people.
    
- **Emotional Regulation (ER):** Managing and controlling one's own emotional states.
    
- **Emotional Empathy (EE):** Sensing and understanding the exact emotions of others.
    
- **Social Expertness (SE):** Navigating complex social situations with skill and ease.
    
- **Trait Empathy (TE):** A broader aspect of empathy that involves a general, baseline tendency to be empathetic across various everyday situations.
    
- **Trait Happiness (TH):** Measuring an individual's characteristic, stable level of happiness.

# UNIT 2 :Personal Competence

## 1. Overview of Personal Competence

Personal competence is divided into two primary sections:

* **Self-awareness:** Observing and recognizing one's own feelings, as well as knowing one's strengths and areas of development.
* **Self-management:** Managing emotions such as anxiety, fear, and anger.

## 2. Deep Dive: Self-Awareness

### Definition

Self-awareness in emotional intelligence refers to the ability to recognize and understand your own emotions, strengths, weaknesses, values, and motivations. It involves being completely in tune with your feelings and maintaining a clear perception of how your emotions impact your behavior and decision-making. Developing this skill is a crucial step in enhancing emotional intelligence, as it lays the foundation for effective self-regulation and interpersonal relationships.

### Key Components of Self-Awareness

* **Recognition of Emotions:** Being aware of one's own emotions and accurately identifying them is a fundamental component of self-awareness.
* **Understanding Emotions:** Going beyond mere recognition to understand the root causes, triggers, and broader implications of one's emotions contributes to a deeper level of self-awareness.
* **Emotional Autobiography:** Reflecting on personal experiences and the associated emotions helps in gaining insights into patterns of emotional responses.
* **Body Awareness:** Paying attention to physical sensations linked to emotions, such as tension or relaxation, contributes to a holistic understanding of emotional states.
* **Mindfulness:** Practicing mindfulness involves staying present in the moment without judgment, allowing individuals to observe their thoughts and emotions objectively.
* **Emotional Honesty:** Acknowledging and accepting one's true feelings, even if they may be uncomfortable, is crucial for genuine self-awareness.
* **Self-Reflection:** Regularly taking time to reflect on personal values, goals, and how emotions align with these aspects enhances self-awareness.
* **Feedback Processing:** Being open to feedback from others and using it as a tool for self-reflection contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of one's emotional impact on others.
* **Emotional Regulation:** Developing the ability to manage and regulate emotions effectively is an essential part of self-awareness.
* **Goal Alignment:** Understanding how emotions align with personal and professional goals helps in making informed decisions and navigating challenges.
## 3. Deep Dive: Self-Management

### Definition

Self-management in emotional intelligence refers to the ability to recognize, understand, and regulate one's own emotions effectively. It involves being aware of your emotional state, managing your impulses, and maintaining a balanced, composed demeanor even in challenging situations.

Self-management is crucial for making sound decisions, handling stress, and maintaining positive relationships both personally and professionally. It includes core skills such as emotional regulation, adaptability, resilience, and the ability to stay focused on long-term goals despite short-term challenges. Developing these skills is key to fostering emotional intelligence and achieving overall success.

### Components of Self-Management

* **Self-Awareness:** Recognizing and understanding one's own emotions, strengths, weaknesses, and values.
* **Self-Regulation:** Managing and controlling one's emotions, impulses, and reactions effectively.
* **Motivation:** Channeling emotions towards personal goals, maintaining enthusiasm, and persisting in the face of setbacks.
* **Empathy:** Understanding and considering others' emotions, perspectives, and feelings.
* **Social Skills:** Building and maintaining healthy relationships, utilizing effective communication, and resolving conflicts positively.

# Unit 3: Social Competence

## 1. Overview of Social Competence

Social competence focuses on how individuals navigate external social environments and manage interpersonal relationships. It is divided into two primary disciplines:

* **Social Awareness:** Focusing on others' perspectives, empathy, and compassion.
* **Relationship Management:** Encompassing effective communication, collaboration, teamwork, and conflict management.

## 2. Deep Dive: Social Awareness

### Definition

Social awareness in emotional intelligence refers to the ability to understand and recognize the emotions of others, as well as being aware of the dynamics in a social situation. It involves perceiving and comprehending the feelings, needs, and concerns of those around you. Socially aware individuals are attuned to the emotional cues and non-verbal signals of others, allowing them to navigate social interactions more effectively.

### Key Components of Social Awareness

* **Empathy:** The capacity to genuinely understand and share the feelings of others. Empathetic individuals can grasp different perspectives and respond with compassion.
* **Social Perception:** The ability to accurately interpret social cues—such as body language, facial expressions, and tone of voice—to understand the emotional states of people in a given situation.
* **Organizational Awareness:** Recognizing and understanding the social dynamics within groups and organizations. This involves an awareness of the formal and informal structures, power relationships, and group norms.
* **Service Orientation:** A mindset that involves anticipating, recognizing, and meeting the needs of others. Individuals high in service orientation are inclined to contribute to the well-being of others.
* **Cultural Awareness:** Being sensitive to and respectful of cultural differences in emotional expression and social norms. This is crucial for effective communication and collaboration in diverse environments.

## 3. Others Perspective, Empathy, and Compassion

### Others Perspective

To properly understand the perspective of others, individuals must:

* Be open minded
* Burst Myths
* Cultivate curiosity

### Empathy

Empathy is the ability to understand and share the feelings of another person. It involves putting oneself in someone else's shoes, comprehending their emotions, and responding with sensitivity. Empathy goes beyond simply recognizing the emotions of others; it also involves experiencing a sense of connection and compassion for what others are going through.

### Compassion

Compassion is a deep awareness of and sympathy for another's suffering, along with a desire to alleviate that suffering. It goes beyond mere empathy, as it involves a genuine and active commitment to understanding and helping others in times of difficulty or distress. Compassion often includes kindness, empathy, and a willingness to take positive action to support and comfort those who are experiencing pain or hardship.

## 4. Deep Dive: Relationship Management

### Definition

Relationship management in the context of emotional intelligence involves effectively and skillfully managing interpersonal interactions to build and maintain positive connections with others. It encompasses the ability to communicate clearly, resolve conflicts, and foster collaborative and mutually beneficial relationships.
