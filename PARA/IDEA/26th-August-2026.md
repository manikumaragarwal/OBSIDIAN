# 26th-August-2026

I've picked up this weird habit that's somehow stopped me from being jealous of people who seem to have it all on social media.

I scroll through their profile.

Not their latest video.

I scroll all the way down.

Like, 100s and 100s of reels...

just to find their first ever video.

And when I finally find it...

it's usually terrible.

They're using filler words,

their sentences are abrupt,

there are these really long pauses,

sometimes you can almost see the hint of ChatGPT in the script...

and their expressions are just... awkward.

<clip of creator's first video>

But I don't watch these videos to laugh at them.

I actually love watching them because it makes me realise...

this is where they started.

And if I have to compare myself to them,

this is the version of them I should be comparing myself to.

Because the version of them that I see today—

the person who's confident on camera,

who knows exactly what to say,

who can write a great hook,

who can hold your attention for 60 seconds—

that person didn't just appear one day.

There are probably hundreds of videos between that awkward first reel

and the person I see today.

Hundreds of times they had to sound awkward.

Hundreds of times they probably made something nobody watched.

Hundreds of times they probably thought,

"maybe I'm just not good at this."

And I think we forget that.

We look at someone's current version,

and then look at ourselves,

and wonder why we're not there yet.

But that's not really a fair comparison.

Because we're comparing our beginning

to someone else's years of hard work.

So now, whenever I find myself comparing myself to someone who's really good at what they do...

I do the same thing.

I scroll down.

All the way down.

To their first video.

<scrolling through reels>

Because maybe that's the person I should be comparing myself to.

Not the person they became...

but the person they were

when they started.
