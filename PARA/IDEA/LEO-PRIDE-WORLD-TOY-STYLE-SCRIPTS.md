Leo’s Pride: The Watering Hole Is Gone
Scene 1: No Water
Bebe runs toward the Watering Hole at full speed and jumps in without looking. Instead of a splash, she lands belly-first in mud. Leo, Kongo, Biko and Kaya arrive behind her and stop when they see that the Watering Hole is almost empty.
BEBE: “...Me.”
Biko pulls Bebe out while Leo looks around the empty Watering Hole. Kongo walks over to the small stream that normally brings the water in and touches the mud.
KONGO: “Wait. It’s still wet.”
Leo comes over and looks at the dry stream.
LEO: “So something’s blocking the water?”
Kongo looks upstream.
KONGO: “Looks like it.”
Leo immediately starts walking.
LEO: “Come on.”
Bebe follows them, still completely covered in mud.
BEBE: “Can I wash first?”
LEO: “When we find the water.”
BEBE: “Fair.”
They follow the dry stream upstream.
Scene 2: They Find the Problem
They walk along the empty stream bed. Leo is ahead of everyone when he suddenly stops. Everyone goes quiet. They can hear running water somewhere nearby.
LEO: “Shh.”
Bebe looks around.
BEBE: “I hear it.”
She runs toward the sound. The others follow and find a huge fallen tree blocking the stream. A large pool of water has built up behind it, while some water is slipping around the side and flowing away from the Watering Hole.
Leo looks at the tree, then at Biko.
LEO: “We move it.”
Kongo walks closer and looks at all the water trapped behind the tree.
KONGO: “Hold on.”
Leo is already pushing against the trunk.
LEO: “Biko?”
Biko joins him.
BIKO: “Yep.”
They push. Nothing happens. Bebe joins them and pushes too, but the tree does not move.
BEBE: “Why is this tree so strong?”
Leo steps back and gets ready again.
LEO: “Again.”
Kongo keeps looking at the water behind the tree.
KONGO: “Leo...”
Leo and Biko push harder. The tree moves slightly.
KONGO: “Wait.”
LEO: “Almost.”
They push again. The tree suddenly rolls away.
Scene 3: Fixed... For About Five Seconds
Water starts flowing back into the old stream and toward the Watering Hole. Bebe cheers and runs beside it.
BEBE: “Yes!”
Leo looks at Kongo.
LEO: “See?”
Kongo is not looking at Leo. The large pool behind the tree is emptying very quickly. The small stream becomes bigger, then much bigger.
Leo turns around.
LEO: “Oh.”
A large rush of water comes toward them.
LEO: “That’s bad.”
Kongo turns and starts running.
KONGO: “Very.”
Everyone runs back toward the Watering Hole. Bebe starts falling behind.
BEBE: “Biko!”
Biko stops. Bebe climbs onto his back.
BEBE: “Go!”
Biko runs with her.
Scene 4: Too Much Water
They reach the Watering Hole just before the rush. Leo runs into the clearing while Kaya starts moving the smaller animals away from the edge.
LEO: “Everybody back!”
The water arrives and pours into the empty Watering Hole. Everyone watches it fill.
BEBE: “It’s back!”
Leo smiles, but the water keeps rising. It reaches the normal level and continues to rise.
LEO: “Okay. That’s enough.”
The water keeps coming.
LEO: “I said enough.”
Kongo looks at him.
LEO: “I know.”
Water begins spilling out of the Watering Hole and into the area around it. Bebe spots a branch floating past and climbs onto it.
BEBE: “Boat!”
The branch starts carrying her away.
LEO: “Bebe!”
Kaya steps into the water and stops the branch with one foot.
BEBE: “I was sailing.”
KAYA: “You were leaving.”
Kaya lifts her off.
Scene 5: Leo Tries to Stop It
Leo sees water spilling over one side of the Watering Hole and starts moving rocks into the gap. Biko joins him, and together they build a small wall.
LEO: “Rocks. Block it.”
The water stops flowing through that spot. Leo steps back.
LEO: “There.”
Water immediately starts going around the other side.
LEO: “...There too.”
They move more rocks and block that side as well. For a moment, it works. Then the water starts building up behind the wall.
Kaya watches one of the rocks begin to shake.
KAYA: “Leo.”
Leo turns and sees the water rising behind the rocks.
LEO: “Oh, come on.”
One rock pops out and sprays him in the face. Then the whole wall breaks and water rushes through.
Bebe’s floating branch passes them again.
BEBE: “My boat!”
LEO: “Forget the boat!”
Bebe watches it float away.
BEBE: “Bye, boat.”
Scene 6: Kongo Finds Another Way
Everyone moves to higher ground while the Watering Hole continues to overflow. Leo looks at the water spreading around them.
LEO: “So we can’t stop it.”
Kongo looks around and notices a dry ditch beside the Watering Hole, filled with leaves, mud, rocks and old branches. He clears some leaves and realizes the ditch leads away from the Watering Hole.
KONGO: “Kaya. What’s this?”
Kaya comes over and looks at it.
KAYA: “Water used to flow here.”
Leo comes over.
LEO: “Can we open it again?”
Kongo looks at the overflowing Watering Hole, then at the old ditch.
KONGO: “Yeah.”
Leo grabs a branch blocking the ditch, then stops and looks at Kongo.
LEO: “This one?”
KONGO: “This one.”
Leo pulls it out. A little water starts flowing into the ditch.
LEO: “Okay. Keep going.”
Everyone starts clearing the old path. Biko rolls the heavy rocks out of the way. Kaya pushes a large log aside. Leo clears smaller branches and mud while Kongo moves ahead, checking that the ditch actually leads away from the Watering Hole.
Bebe picks up one tiny stick and carries it to the pile beside Biko.
BEBE: “Done.”
Biko looks at the tiny stick.
BIKO: “Big help.”
BEBE: “I know.”
She goes back for another one.
They clear most of the ditch, but one large pile of mud and branches is still blocking the entrance. Leo pushes it, but it does not move. Biko joins him and it shifts slightly. Kaya comes over as the water behind them keeps rising.
Kongo checks the old ditch.
KONGO: “One more.”
Leo looks at Biko and Kaya.
LEO: “Ready?”
Biko nods. Kaya lowers her head.
LEO: “Go!”
All three push. The blockage breaks apart and water rushes into the old ditch.
Everyone steps back and watches. The extra water flows safely away from the Watering Hole. The level finally stops rising.
Bebe grabs a leaf and drops it into the water.
BEBE: “Test.”
The leaf floats into the old ditch and continues downstream.
BEBE: “It worked!”
Kongo watches the water flowing properly. His forehead mark begins to glow.
Leo looks at Kongo.
LEO: “Nice.”
Kongo looks at him.
KONGO: “Nice?”
Leo smiles.
LEO: “Really nice.”
Scene 7: Bebe Gets Her Swim
Later, the Watering Hole is full and calm again. Everyone is resting nearby when Bebe starts slowly walking backwards.
Leo notices immediately.
LEO: “No.”
Bebe keeps going.
LEO: “Bebe.”
She crouches.
LEO: “Don’t you—”
Bebe runs and jumps.
HUGE SPLASH.
Leo gets completely soaked.
Bebe surfaces.
BEBE: “Much better.”
Leo stands there dripping for a moment, then looks at Bebe and starts running toward the water.
BEBE: “Wait—”
Leo jumps in.
SPLASH.
Bebe starts laughing.
END.

Leo’s Pride: Who Took All the Fruit?
Setting: Fruit Grove
Target: ~7 minutes
Characters: Leo (lion), Bebe (hippo), Kongo (gorilla), Biko (buffalo), Kaya (rhino), Tamu (vervet monkey) and other vervet monkeys
Fruits: Marula, masuku, monkey orange
Scene 1: The Last Fruit
A small hand reaches down from a low branch and grabs the last marula fruit from a huge leaf mat that is completely empty. It belongs to Tamu (vervet monkey). In the background, Leo (lion) and Bebe (hippo) are walking back toward the spot carrying another load of fruit. Bebe is struggling with one big monkey orange. Tamu disappears into the leaves just before Leo looks up and sees the empty mat.
BEBE: “Why did we pick the biggest one?”
LEO: “Where’s all our fruit?”
Bebe drops the monkey orange and runs over. Leo looks around the empty collection spot. One marula rolls out from behind a nearby tree. Leo follows it. As he reaches down, Tamu’s hand reaches for the same fruit. Leo grabs it first. Tamu freezes on the branch above him. Leo looks up. They stare at each other for a second. Tamu grabs Leo’s paw with one hand, snatches the marula with the other and races up the tree.
LEO: “Hey!”
Bebe looks up as Tamu disappears through the branches.
BEBE: “Found the fruit.”
LEO: “And who took it.”
Scene 2: Catch Tamu
Kongo (gorilla), Biko (buffalo) and Kaya (rhino) join them. Leo puts three marula fruits on the leaf mat and tells everyone to hide. Leo and Kongo squeeze behind one tree. Kaya stands completely still beside another. Biko gets behind a bush that barely covers his legs. Bebe crouches behind a tiny plant with almost her entire body visible.
LEO: “Bebe.”
BEBE: “What?”
LEO: “I can see you.”
Bebe looks at the tiny plant in front of her.
BEBE: “I can’t.”
Leo gives up. Tamu slowly climbs down from the tree, checks both sides and reaches for one marula. Leo jumps out. Tamu grabs the fruit and takes off.
LEO: “Got you!”
Tamu jumps onto a branch and throws the marula to another vervet monkey in the next tree.
LEO: “Or not.”
Leo runs after the second vervet monkey. It throws the fruit to a third.
BEBE: “They’re passing it!”
LEO: “I noticed!”
Leo and Bebe chase the marula through the grove while Tamu and the other vervet monkeys pass it from tree to tree. Behind them, two more vervet monkeys climb down beside Biko and start taking the other bait fruits. Biko turns. One vervet monkey freezes with two masuku fruits in its hands. Biko stares at it. The vervet monkey slowly puts one fruit back down. Biko nods. It runs away with the other one. Tamu immediately drops from another branch, grabs the fruit that was left behind and disappears again.
BIKO: “Hmm.”
Scene 3: Everything Is Gone
Leo finally gets ahead of Tamu and blocks the path between two trees. Tamu stops with the marula in his hand.
LEO: “Nowhere to go.”
Tamu looks behind Leo. Leo turns to check. Tamu runs straight past him.
BEBE: “Ha!”
LEO: “Not funny.”
They return to the collection spot. All three bait fruits are gone too. Leo stares at the empty leaf mat while Biko points above them. Tamu and several other vervet monkeys are moving deeper into the grove carrying marula, masuku and monkey oranges.
BEBE: “They’re really good at this.”
Leo starts following them. The others come with him. Every few trees, one of the vervet monkeys drops a leaf or a small fruit, so the group follows the trail deeper into the grove. Eventually they reach a quieter area and stop. Tamu and the other vervet monkeys are sitting around several much younger vervet monkeys, sharing the fruit with them. One little vervet monkey is trying to lift a monkey orange almost as big as its head. It falls backward, gets up and tries again. Bebe watches.
BEBE: “Oh.”
Leo looks around. Most of the nearby trees have almost no ripe fruit left. He looks back toward the part of the Fruit Grove they came from, where there is still plenty. Then he turns around.
LEO: “Come on.”
BEBE: “We’re leaving?”
LEO: “No.”
Scene 4: A Bigger Pile
Leo goes back to the trees with plenty of fruit and starts collecting again. Bebe understands and joins him. Kongo checks each fruit before picking it. Kaya collects ripe fruit that has already fallen. Biko carries a huge pile of marula and masuku against his chest. Tamu watches them from a tree. Kaya picks up a fallen marula and places it in the pile. Tamu looks down, then copies her. Soon the other vervet monkeys start helping too.
Leo reaches for a fruit and Kongo stops him.
KONGO: “Not that one.”
Leo squeezes it. It is still hard.
LEO: “Right.”
Bebe keeps adding tiny fruits to the massive load Biko is already carrying.
BEBE: “One more.”
BIKO: “No.”
Bebe adds one.
BEBE: “Last one.”
She adds another. Tamu and Leo reach for the same marula. They both stop. Leo points toward another side of the tree.
LEO: “You take that side.”
Tamu looks where Leo pointed, then grabs the marula Leo was reaching for and runs.
LEO: “That wasn’t your side.”
Bebe laughs. The new fruit pile gets bigger and bigger. Then Leo notices a marula tree covered with ripe yellow fruit.
LEO: “That one.”
Scene 5: Too Much Fruit
Biko stands beside the marula tree while Leo looks up at all the fruit.
LEO: “Just shake it a little.”
Biko pushes the trunk. A few marula fruits fall. Everyone quickly picks them up.
BEBE: “Again!”
Biko shakes the tree a little harder. A lot more marula falls. Bebe runs underneath trying to catch them while Tamu and the other vervet monkeys grab whatever lands in the branches.
BEBE: “Again!”
Kongo looks up. The tree is still full.
KONGO: “That’s probably enough.”
Leo looks at the tree, then at the huge pile they have collected.
LEO: “One more.”
Kongo looks at him.
LEO: “Little one.”
Biko gives the tree one more shake. Hundreds of marula fruits drop at once. Everyone ducks. Fruit bounces across the ground and crashes into their big collection pile. A monkey orange rolls out from the bottom. It hits another fruit. Then another. The entire pile shifts and starts collapsing down the slope.
LEO: “Oh no.”
Bebe watches dozens of fruits begin rolling downhill.
BEBE: “That’s a lot.”
Leo starts running after them.
LEO: “Go!”
Scene 6: The Fruit Chase
Marula, masuku and monkey oranges roll between the trees. Leo, Tamu and the other vervet monkeys race downhill after them while Bebe tries grabbing whatever comes near her. She catches a monkey orange and holds it up proudly.
BEBE: “Got one!”
A marula hits her foot. She drops the monkey orange and it rolls away again.
BEBE: “Had one!”
Biko runs ahead and plants himself in front of the rolling fruit. Several marula bounce off him. Then several more. Then a monkey orange hits his leg. Biko steps aside.
BIKO: “Too many.”
The fruit keeps rolling. At the bottom of the slope is a large muddy patch. Leo sees where everything is heading.
LEO: “They’re going into the mud!”
Kongo stops chasing. He watches the fruit instead. A large rock in the middle of the slope is splitting the fruit into two paths. Some rolls toward the mud, while the rest rolls toward a shallow grassy hollow beside it. Kongo watches one marula hit the rock and turn safely toward the grass. Then another does the same.
KONGO: “Leo!”
Leo runs back toward him.
LEO: “What?”
Kongo points toward the two paths.
KONGO: “Don’t stop them.”
Leo looks at the grassy hollow.
LEO: “Turn them.”
Kongo nods.
Scene 7: Turn the Fruit
Kongo points to a long fallen branch near the muddy path. Biko grabs it and drags it across the path leading toward the mud. Kaya braces one end of the branch while Leo, Tamu and the other vervet monkeys quickly add smaller branches and rocks beside it.
KONGO: “Biko. There.”
The first group of marula hits the branch and changes direction, rolling safely toward the grassy hollow.
BEBE: “Again!”
More fruit comes down. The barrier keeps turning it away from the mud. Then everyone hears a louder rolling sound. They look uphill. One enormous monkey orange is bouncing directly toward them.
BEBE: “That one’s huge.”
The monkey orange crashes into the barrier. The branch moves. Kaya digs her feet into the ground. Biko grabs the other end. Leo pushes against the middle while Tamu and the other vervet monkeys quickly shove more rocks behind it. For a moment, the barrier almost gives way. Then the monkey orange turns and rolls safely into the grassy hollow.
Everyone watches it stop.
BEBE: “We got it!”
The grassy hollow is now filled with the fruit they saved. Kongo looks at the path, the barrier and all the fruit sitting safely away from the mud. The chevron on his forehead starts glowing.
LEO: “Nice.”
Kongo looks at him.
KONGO: “Nice?”
Leo looks at the enormous pile.
LEO: “Really nice.”
Scene 8: Too Slow
Later, Leo’s group and Tamu’s group sit together around the fruit in the grassy hollow. The younger vervet monkeys eat beside everyone instead of hiding in the trees. Leo reaches for a marula at the same time as one small vervet monkey. They both stop. Leo pushes the marula toward the little vervet monkey. It takes it, then picks up a monkey orange and rolls it toward Leo. Leo smiles and reaches for it. Bebe dives in and grabs it first.
BEBE: “Thanks!”
Leo looks at her.
LEO: “That was for me.”
Bebe is already walking away with the monkey orange.
BEBE: “Too slow.”
Leo looks at Tamu. Tamu looks back at Leo. Tamu laughs. Leo starts laughing too.
