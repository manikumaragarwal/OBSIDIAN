# 6 psychological priciples for content!

### MERE EXPOSURE EFFECT!

> **familiarity beats originality every single time**

1. your brain decides before you do.

- have I seen this before and enjoyed?
- something unexpected happening?
- does this have anything to do with me and my life?

- new format -> brain doesn't get excited!!

- that's why original content fails so often!!!

#### THE FORMAT STEAL

see what's going viral! and plug your channel in that format!

> [!CAUTION] beware not to use the formats that are not from your niches!
> viewer might question your credibility!!

### Curiosity Traps!!

- never lead with the main meat of the video!!
- tease the curiosity!! if it's very VERY FAR! the viewer might just skip!!!
- delaying the reward

  -example: let's say, you're watching something dirty on the internet!! as soon as you're done with your deeds! you'll immediately close the video.. grab the tissue... and hop back on instagram or youtube..

  - but if it's solved completely even before half the video is played... you'll just skip after getting that... rest of the video is not important! :
    - either stretch the curiosity gap!,
    - add another conflict
    - cut the rest of the clip! (_it'll just decrease your viewer retention, hence less algorithmic pushing!_)

### Zeigarnik Effect ('scene-cuts')

example: in any movies... you might have seen something bad is about to happen to one character!! you're about to get something interesting.. but the camera cuts to some other character in some other location! (find a concrete example in very famous movie or shows)

### Hitchcock's Bomb (suspense v/s surprise)

a guy is cheating on her wife! and the girlfriend finds out from her friend that some girl came to your house!! now the girlfriend gets in her car and drives towards the house!!  
now the camera cuts to the two infedel couples having their fun!! you'll have this sense of lingering danger!! and the tension will arise in your brain even when they are having fun!!

another example: "SONIC MOVIE" how did I get here! .. you watch the whole story analyzing what went wrong!!

### Chekhov's Gun (environemental anomaly)

The human eye is drawn to anomalies. When a viewer spots something that doesn't quite fit the standard "talking head" setup, they will hold their attention on the video simply to see if the anomaly is addressed. It creates a secondary curiosity loop entirely separate from the audio you are speaking.

example: CUM(in mino lee's video, the mathematic formulae in the 'how to speak better' video, and other things)
