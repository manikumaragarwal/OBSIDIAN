This was an essay written by [[Johnathan Swift]] in the 17th Century. 
It is a [[Juvenalian Satire|Juvenalian]](dark comedy) [[Satire in Literature|satirical]] social commentary on the Food shortage related issue in the Ireland at that time.

Poor people of Ireland had too many babies, and they couldn't afford to properly feed them, and the growing infant population was a burden for the country's growth.  It was a trouble, so Swift gives this Proposal that, 
> We'll feed the baby till 14 months, and when they're plump and juicy, we'll eat them.. this will solve the infant overpopulation problem, and will Give the babies to become an active participating member of the society.  They won't be a burden anymore. 

out of the 120 thousand infants in Ireland, they will save 20 thousand for the breeding purpose(*1/4 consisting of male babies, rest females... considering a single male can have baby with 4 females*) and rest 100 thousands will be used for feast.

> [!NOTE] Satire as a Political Tool
> Swift's essay is [[Juvenalian Satire]] — the harshest, most biting form of satire. Compare with:
> - [[Lysistrata - Aristophanes|Lysistrata]] — Aristophanes uses comedy and obscenity to critique political authority; Swift uses mock-rational argument
> - Both use laughter (or shock) to make political critiques that direct speech cannot
> See [[Satire in Literature]] for the broader theory.

### Level 1 
- Things are bad in Ireland, especially the children.
- The name itself is satirical.... "Modest", meaning no one is going to be offended by the Proposal. 
> "It is true, a child just dropped from its dam may be supported by her milk for a solar year, with little other nourishment"
	- the use of word "dam"(mother animal) represents, how swift was seeing poor people as **not** human, but an animal.

- Swift starts his satiric approach, by first telling us about the problems, and later about the solution.
- Infants can be used not only for the food, but their skin can be flayed, and it will be used as an expensive leather for gloves and boots for the Riches.
- 

### Level 2 
Without actually saying it, swift raises a fundamental question : 
> "***Which is worse, to eat a child before the age of 1?  or let a Child grow Homeless, and end into Ruins*"

- This satire talks about the Homeless people. "*Why do we have a problem with killing a 1 year old baby who wouldn't even feel pain, but we're completely okay with ignoring people who sleep empty-bellied(famished) on the streets.*"

> [!TIP] Compare: Moral Shock as Technique
> - Swift shocks the reader into confronting society's indifference to poverty
> - [[TARA play|Tara]] uses a different kind of shock — the revelation of what was done to Tara's body forces the audience to confront gender-based medical violence
> - [[Mahabharata|Draupadi's question]] in the dice game similarly uses a moment of moral shock to expose the system's incoherence
