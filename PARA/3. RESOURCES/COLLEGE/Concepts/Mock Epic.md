# Mock Epic

A mock epic (or mock-heroic) applies the grand conventions of classical epic poetry — invocations, divine machinery, epic similes, formal diction — to a trivial or mundane subject, creating comic incongruity.

## Key Features

- Elevated language for lowly subjects
- Epic catalogues, invocations, and battles over trifles
- [[Irony]] through the gap between form and content
- Implicit moral critique of the values being satirised

## Appears In

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — the definitive English mock epic
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — mock-heroic elements in Lilliput's wars

## Examples

- Belinda's dressing table as an "altar" with sacred rites
- The card game in Canto III rendered as an epic battle
- Sylphs performing epic guard duties over a curl of hair
