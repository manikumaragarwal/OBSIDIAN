# Human Nature in Swift

[[Jonathan Swift]]'s view of human nature is radically pessimistic. He questions the [[Enlightenment Thought|Enlightenment]] premise that humans are essentially rational animals.

> [!NOTE] Human Nature Across Tragedies
> Different texts explore human nature through different lenses:
> - **Swift**: Humans are driven by pride, not reason — physical embodiment undermines dignity
> - **[[Macbeth - Themes|Macbeth]]**: Ambition is the core human flaw — unchecked it destroys both self and order
> - **[[Mahabharata]]**: Dharma (duty) versus inherent desire — the eternal struggle within human nature
> - **[[Doctor Faustus - Christopher Marlowe|Doctor Faustus]]**: Human desire for knowledge/power exceeds moral limits

## Key Arguments

- Humans are not *animal rationale* (rational animal) but *rationis capax* (merely capable of reason)
- Reason is commonly used to rationalise vice, not to achieve virtue
- Physical embodiment (eating, defecating, copulating) undermines pretensions to dignity
- Pride is the master vice — the conviction that we are better than we are

## In Gulliver's Travels

- **Lilliput** — petty ambition drives political life → [[Political Satire]]
- **Brobdingnag** — seen at close range, human bodies are disgusting
- **Laputa** — reason without practical judgement is absurd → [[Rationalism vs Absurdity]]
- **Houyhnhnms** — rationality without passion produces cold perfection; Yahoos embody humanity stripped of illusions
