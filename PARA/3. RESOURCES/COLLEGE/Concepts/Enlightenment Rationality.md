# Enlightenment Rationality

Enlightenment rationality refers to the eighteenth-century confidence that human reason — independent of tradition, revelation, or superstition — could discover truth and improve society.

> [!TIP] Reason and Its Limits Across Traditions
> The confidence in human reason has been questioned across different literary traditions:
> - **18th Century**: Swift exposes reason's abuse (Laputans) and questions if humans are truly rational
> - **[[Aristotle's Poetics]]**: Greek tragedy assumes humans are rational beings capable of choice — essential to the concept of hamartia
> - **[[Doctor Faustus - Christopher Marlowe]]**: Reason leads Faustus to damnation — knowledge without moral restraint
> - **[[Macbeth - William Shakespeare|Macbeth]]**: Macbeth's rational calculation of ambition leads to psychological destruction

## Key Ideas

- Reason as the universal standard of judgement
- Empirical observation over inherited dogma
- Rational self-interest as the basis of social order
- Progress through education and science

## In Literature

- [[Alexander Pope]] upholds rational moderation ("the proper study of mankind is Man")
- [[Jonathan Swift]] attacks the *abuse* of reason (Laputans, projectors) without abandoning it entirely
- The gap between rational ideals and irrational behaviour drives much [[Satire]]

## Critiques

- Swift questions whether humans are truly rational ([[Human Nature in Swift]])
- Sentiment and feeling also claim moral authority ([[PARA/3. RESOURCES/COLLEGE/Concepts/Reason vs Emotion]])
