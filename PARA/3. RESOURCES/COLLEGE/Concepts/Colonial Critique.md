# Colonial Critique

Colonial critique in eighteenth-century literature interrogates the moral, political, and human costs of European imperial expansion.

> [!NOTE] Power Structures Across Texts
> The theme of unequal power and exploitation appears in different forms:
> - **18th Century Empire**: Swift dismantles European justification for conquest through the Houyhnhnm perspective
> - **[[Mahabharata]]**: Draupadi's question in the dice game exposes how patriarchal authority enables exploitation
> - **[[TARA play|Tara]]**: Medical authority and patriarchal family combine to enable gender-based violence

## Key Concerns

- Whether "civilised" Europeans are truly superior to colonised peoples
- The violence and exploitation underpinning empire
- The complicity of commerce and Christianity in colonial projects
- Alternative perspectives from non-European viewpoints

## In the Texts

- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — Gulliver's speech to his Houyhnhnm master about European conquests is devastatingly ironic; the King of Brobdingnag declares Europeans "odious vermin"
- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — imperial trade goods woven into everyday luxury
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — indirect engagement through economic precarity linked to global markets
