# Irony

Irony is the expression of meaning through language that normally signifies the opposite, or a discrepancy between appearance and reality, expectation and outcome.

> [!TIP] Irony Across Literature
> Irony operates differently across texts and periods:
> - **[[PRIDE AND PREJUDICE|Pride and Prejudice]]**: Austen's signature technique, the opening line's inversion reveals women's agency in the marriage market
> - **[[Macbeth - William Shakespeare|Macbeth]]**: Dramatic irony- audience knows prophecies that characters misunderstand; appearance vs. reality as central paradox
> - **18th Century**: Pope's elevating language applied to trivial hair-cutting; Swift's naïve narrator masking bitter contempt

## Types

- **Verbal irony** — saying one thing, meaning another
- **Situational irony** — events contradicting expectations
- **Dramatic irony** — audience knows what characters do not
- **Structural irony** — sustained through an unreliable narrator
