# Heroic Couplet

The heroic couplet is a pair of rhyming iambic pentameter lines, the dominant verse form of [[18th Century English Literature]].

## Features

- Ten syllables per line (iambic pentameter)
- End-rhymed in pairs (AABB)
- Usually end-stopped (self-contained thought in each couplet)
- Allows for balance, antithesis, and epigrammatic wit

## Master Practitioner

[[Alexander Pope]] perfected the heroic couplet, using it for [[Satire]], philosophy, and narrative in works like [[The Rape of the Lock]], *The Dunciad*, and *An Essay on Man*.

## Example

> "What dire offence from am'rous causes springs, / What mighty contests rise from trivial things" — [[The Rape of the Lock]], I.1–2
