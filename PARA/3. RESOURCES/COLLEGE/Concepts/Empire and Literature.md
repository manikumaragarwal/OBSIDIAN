# Empire and Literature

Eighteenth-century English literature was produced within and against an expanding imperial project. The relationship is complex: some texts celebrated empire, many more encoded its assumptions silently, and a few mounted searching critiques.

## How Literature and Empire Intersect

- **Travel writing as imperial genre** — voyages of discovery produced the conventions Swift parodies in *Gulliver's Travels*; the traveller figure naturalises European perspective as universal
- **Luxury goods as textual presence** — imported commodities (silks, spices, gems) appear constantly in 18th-century poetry, encoding empire as domestic pleasure → [[Capital and Trade]]
- **The "savage" as mirror** — non-Europeans function in satire as foils for critique: the King of Brobdingnag, the Houyhnhnms, Goldsmith's *Citizen of the World* — all deploy the outside gaze to expose European folly
- **Absence of the colonised** — the actual violence of empire is more often suppressed than represented; its wealth is silently present

## Key Arguments

- **Postcolonial reading** — texts like *Gulliver's Travels* are both anti-imperialist (explicit colonial critique) and complicit (they naturalise European centrality)
- **[[Cosmopolitanism]]** — some writers appeal to universal humanity to challenge imperial hierarchy
- **[[Enlightenment Rationality]]** — "civilising mission" rhetoric used Enlightenment reason to justify conquest

## In the Texts

- **[[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]]** — the most sustained literary colonial critique of the period; Gulliver's speech to his Houyhnhnm master (Part IV) demolishes European self-congratulation; the King of Brobdingnag's verdict: "the most pernicious race of little odious vermin"
- **[[The Rape of the Lock]]** — empire is present as *consumer goods*, not as violence; "glittering spoil" of conquest adorns Belinda's dressing table
- **[[The Vicar of Wakefield]]** — economic vulnerability tied to global market forces that lie beyond the characters' comprehension or control
