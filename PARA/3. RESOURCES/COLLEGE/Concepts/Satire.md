# Satire

Satire is a literary technique that uses humour, [[Irony]], exaggeration, or ridicule to criticise human behaviour, institutions, or social norms.

> [!NOTE] Satire Across Literature
> Satire appears across different eras and cultures as a mode of political and social critique:
> - **18th Century**: Swift's [[Political Satire]] and Pope's gentle mockery
> - **Greek Comedy**: [[Lysistrata - Aristophanes]] uses sexual comedy to critique war and patriarchy
> - **Enlightenment**: [[A Modest Proposal - Johnathan Swift|"A Modest Proposal"]] employs [[Juvenalian Satire]] to shock readers into confronting social injustice
>
> See [[Satire in Literature]] for broader theory.

## Key Features

- Exaggeration and caricature
- [[Irony]] and wit
- Moral critique behind the comedy
- Social commentary aimed at reform or exposure

## Types

- **Horatian satire** — gentle, witty, urbane (Pope)
- **Juvenalian satire** — harsh, bitter, indignant (Swift)
- **Menippean satire** — mixing prose and verse, attacking attitudes

## Appears In

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — Horatian satire of vanity
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — Juvenalian satire of politics and humanity
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — gentle ironic satire of naïveté
