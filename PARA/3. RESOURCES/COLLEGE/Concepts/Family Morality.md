# Family Morality

The family as a moral unit — a site of virtue, duty, loyalty, and emotional education — is a central concern in [[18th Century English Literature]], especially in the [[Sentimental Novel]].

> [!NOTE] Family and Duty Across Texts
> Different traditions explore family as both moral centre and source of conflict:
> - **18th Century**: The family as domestic virtue tested by economic hardship and aristocratic exploitation
> - **[[Mahabharata]]**: Family torn between dharma (duty) and familial bonds — the dicing game destroys both
> - **[[TARA play|Tara]]**: Patriarchal family actively harms daughter to benefit son — family as source of violence
> - **[[PRIDE AND PREJUDICE]]**: Various marriage types explored as family arrangements — pragmatic, emotional, ideal

## Key Ideas

- The family as a microcosm of social order
- Parental duty and filial obedience
- Domestic virtue as the moral foundation of society
- Threats to the family from economic hardship, seduction, and aristocratic exploitation

## In the Texts

- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — the Primrose family's trials test and ultimately vindicate domestic virtue
- [[Dr Primrose]] embodies the ideal of Christian fatherhood
- Olivia's seduction by Squire Thornhill dramatises the fragility of female honour → [[PARA/3. RESOURCES/COLLEGE/Concepts/Gender Politics]]
