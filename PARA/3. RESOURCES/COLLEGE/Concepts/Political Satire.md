# Political Satire

Political satire targets the structures, actors, and absurdities of government and political power. It was a primary mode of [[Satire]] in the eighteenth century.

> [!TIP] Cross-Text Comparisons
> Political satire takes different forms across literature:
> - **[[Lysistrata - Aristophanes]]**: Aristophanes uses comedy and women's body politics to critique Athenian militarism
> - **[[A Modest Proposal - Johnathan Swift]]**: Swift uses mock-rational argument to expose Irish economic exploitation
> - **18th Century Travel Satire**: [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] uses the travel narrative to critique European politics and empire

## Key Features

- Allegorical representations of real politicians and parties
- Exposure of corruption, hypocrisy, and lust for power
- Use of [[Irony]] and indirect attack to evade censorship
- Often linked to specific partisan positions (Tory/Whig)

## Appears In

- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — Lilliput's factions satirise Whig/Tory divisions; rope-dancing = ministerial appointments
- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — oblique commentary on aristocratic feuds with political undertones
