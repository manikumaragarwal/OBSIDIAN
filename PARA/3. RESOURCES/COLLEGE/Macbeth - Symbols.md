# Macbeth — Key Symbols

> [!INFO] Symbol Layering
> Shakespeare uses symbols that operate on multiple levels: literal, metaphorical, thematic, and psychological. This richness makes the play endlessly interpretable.

---

## Blood — The Inescapable Stain

> "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood / Clean from my hand?"

Blood appears **over 100 times** in the play, making it the central symbol.

### Forms Blood Takes

| Symbolic Type | Meaning | Example |
|---------------|---------|---------|
| **Literal blood** | Violence, murder | Duncan's murder, Macduff's family |
| **Blood on hands** | Guilt | "Out, damned spot! ... a little water clears us" |
| **"Blood will have blood"** | Cycle of vengeance | Revenge spawns more revenge |
| **Thick blood** | Psychological horror | "Gouts of blood" on the dagger |
| **"Sewed with blood"** | Corruption | "No son of mine succeeding" |

### The Washing Motif — Development

| Progression | Evidence | Interpretation |
|-------------|----------|----------------|
| **Denial** | Lady M: "A little water clears us of this deed" | She believes guilt is removable |
| **Recognition** | Lady M: "All the perfumes of Arabia will not sweeten" | Guilt is PERMANENT |
| **Acceptance** | Macbeth: "I am in blood / Stepp'd in so far" | Acknowledges the trap |

> [!NOTE] Symbolism of Water vs. Blood
> - **Water** usually means purification, baptism, renewal
> - **In Macbeth**, CANNOT cleanse — it only reveals how deep the stain goes
> - This subverts religious symbolism: **even God's ocean cannot absolve Macbeth**

## Darkness & Light

> "Stars, hide your fires; / Let not light see my black and deep desires."

### The Darkness Symbol

| Aspect | Meaning | Evidence |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **Literal darkness** | Night = time for secrets | Act 1, Scene 5: "Come, thick night" |
| **Metaphorical darkness** | Evil intention | "Black and deep desires" |
| **Psychological darkness** | Madness, confusion | Macbeth's hallucinations in the dark |
| **Cosmic darkness** | Disorder, unnaturalness | Nature goes haywig after Duncan's murder |


### Light as Truth

| Character | Relationship with Light | Meaning |
|-----------|------------------------|---------|
| **Lady Macbeth** | "Come, thick night" | WANTS darkness to hide her intentions |
| **Macbeth** | "Stars, hide your fires" | Needs darkness to conceal his ambition |
| **Duncan** | "This castle hath a pleasant seat" | SEES light and beauty (blindness to betrayal) |

## Sleep — Death of Innocence

> "Macbeth does murder sleep."

### What Sleep Represents

| Meaning | Evidence |
|---------|----------|
| **Restoration** | Time for the soul to heal |
| **Innocence** | Only the guiltless can truly rest |
| **Divine peace** | God grants sleep to the worthy |


### The Death of Sleep

When Macbeth kills Duncan, he also kills sleep for himself and others:

| Consequence | Evidence |
|-------------|----------|
| **Macbeth cannot sleep** | Haunted by hallucinations, paranoia |
| **Lady Macbeth cannot sleep** | Walks in unconscious state, haunted by guilt |
| **"Murder sleep"** | He removes the possibility of peace |

> [!TIP] Connection to Guilt Theme
> Sleeplessness in Shakespeare = **Guilt manifesting physically**. By murdering sleep, Macbeth ensures **eternal insomnia** — his conscience will never rest.

## The Dagger — Hallucinated Guilt

> "Is this a dagger which I see before me, / The handle toward my hand?"

### The Dagger as Symbol

| Layer | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Literal** | Not actually there (hallucination) |
| **Psychological** | Guilt taking physical form |
| **Thematic** | The **irreversible path** Macbeth is choosing |
| **Moral** | Violence manifesting as temptation |

### "Gouts of Blood"

The dagger appears with blood flowing from it:

> "And on thy blade and dudgeon gouts of blood, / Which was not so before."

This symbolizes:
1. **Future violence** — The blood hasn't been spilled yet, but WILL be
2. **Inescapable guilt** — The blood is ALREADY on his mind
3. **Psychological inevitability** — Macbeth's mind is already committing the murder

---

## The Crown — Burden, Not Reward

> "Upon my head they placed a fruitless crown."

### The Crown's Symbolism

| Stage | Meaning of Crown | Evidence |
|-------|------------------|----------|
| **Before murder** | **Temptation**, glory to be won | The thing Macbeth craves |
| **Immediately after** | **Burden**, fits uncomfortably | Macbeth feels uneasy wearing it |
| **Later** | **Source of paranoia** | "To be thus is nothing, / But to be safely thus" |
| **Final** | **Empty power**, meaningless | Life is "a tale... signifying nothing" |

### The "Barren Sceptre"

> "No son of mine succeeding."

The crown is **barren** because Macbeth has no heir. This means:
1. **His reign is temporary**
2. **His ambition is futile** — even if he keeps the crown, his line ends
3. **He killed Duncan in vain** — The restoration of Malcolm is inevitable

## Nature & Animals — Cosmic Disorder

> "Thou seest the heavens, as troubled with man's act, / Threaten his bloody stage."

### Animals Following Duncan's Murder

| Animal | Behavior | Symbolic Meaning |
|--------|----------|------------------|
| **Owl** | Kills a falcon | Predator preys on predator — ORDER INVERTED |
| **Duncan's horses** | Eat each other | Cannibalism — HIGHEST FORM OF DISORDER |
| **Falcon** | Eaten by owl | Noble bird destroyed |
| **Nature** | Earthquakes, storms | The cosmos reacts to regicide |


> [!NOTE] Great Chain of Being
> The Elizabethans believed in the **Great Chain of Being**:
> - God at top, then angels, then humans, then animals, then plants, then minerals
> - Each level had proper order and purpose
> - Killing the king (God's representative) disrupts the ENTIRE chain

By making animals behave unnaturally, Shakespeare shows that **regicide violates cosmic order itself**.

## Birds — Foreshadowing & Status

| Bird | Scene | Symbolic Meaning |
|------|-------|------------------|
| **Martlet** (a bird) | Duncan praises Inverness | Innocence, peace, natural order |
| **Owl** | After Duncan's death | Harbinger of death, disorder |
| **Falcon** | After Duncan's death | Nobility, natural superiority |
| **Crow** | Murder of Duncan | Death, carrion, foulness |

The **progression** from martlet (peace) to crow (death) symbolizes Macbeth's descent from nobility to monstrosity.

## Hands — Action & Guilt

> "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood / Clean from my hand?"

### What Hands Represent

| Hand Imagery | Meaning |
|--------------|---------|
| **"Thy bones are marrowless, thy blood is cold"** | Dead Banquo touching Macbeth |
| **"Out, damned spot!"** | Lady Macbeth seeing blood on her hands |
| **"Give me my armor"** | Macbeth's hands reaching for violence |
| **"Your hand"** | Shake hands as sign of trust, loyalty |


### The Washing Hands Motif

Lady Macbeth tells Macbeth to "wash this filthy witness from your hand." Later, SHE is the one obsessed with washing.

This shows:
1. **Guilt transfer**: The burden moves from Macbeth to Lady Macbeth
2. **Inability to cleanse**: No amount of washing can remove moral stain
3. **Psychological reality**: Guilt cannot be PHYSICALLY removed

## The Porter — Hell's Gate

The Porter scene (2.3) connects to the play's theme of evil:

| Hell Image | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| **"Knock, knock! Who's there?"** | The entrance to hell |
| **Beelzebub** | The devil |
| **"The devil himself"** | References Satan |
| **"Porter of hell-gate"** | Explicitly states connection |

The Porter serves as **comic relief** but also reinforces that **Macbeth has created hell on earth**.

---

## Clothing — Unearned Power

> "Borrow'd robes" = Unfitting authority

| Clothing Metaphor | Meaning | Scene/Character |
|-------------------|---------|-----------------|
| "Why do you dress me in / Borrow'd robes?" | Authority that doesn't fit | Macbeth, when he's told he's Thane of Cawdor |
| "Like a giant's robe / Upon a dwarfish thief" | Power that doesn't belong | Angus, describing Macbeth |

**Interpretation**: Macbeth's kingship is a **stolen garment** — it never truly fits him. He's playing a role that's not his, and everyone can see it.

---

## Weather — Emotional Atmosphere

> "When shall we three meet again? / In thunder, lightning, or in rain?"

| Weather | Meaning | Scene |
|----------|---------|-------|
| **Thunder & lightning** | Chaos, supernatural activity | Witches' first appearance |
| **Unnatural darkness** | Evil, moral corruption | Night of Duncan's murder |
| **Earthquake** | Cosmic disruption | After regicide |

> [!TIP] Jacobean Context
| The original audience would have recognized that **unusual weather** = **God's wrath**. This reinforces the idea that Macbeth has committed a **sacrilege**.

# Symbol by Symbol Quick Reference

| Symbol | First Appearance | Final Reference | Theme |
|--------|------------------|-----------------|-------|
| **Blood** | "gouts of blood" on dagger (2.1) | "Blood will have blood" (3.4) | Guilt, violence, cyclical revenge |
| **Sleep** | "Murder sleep" (2.2) | Lady Macbeth sleep-walking (5.1) | Innocence lost, guilt |
| **Dagger** | Hallucinated dagger (2.1) | No further mentions | Guilt manifesting, temptation |
| **Crown** | "Fruitless crown" (3.1) | "Signifying nothing" (empty) (5.5) | Ambition's futility |
| **Animals** | Fair is foul (1.1) | Nature disrupted after murder (2.4) | Order vs. chaos |
