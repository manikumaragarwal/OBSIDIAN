> [!NOTE] Aristotelian Structure in Tara
> See [[Aristotle's Poetics]] for the tragic plot framework. Tara's structure is **non-linear memory** — but the emotional logic follows [[3 Tragic Beats]]:
> - **Peripeteia**: The revelation that Tara's leg was deliberately given to her brother — the family's lie collapses
> - **Anagnorisis**: Dan's gradual discovery (through writing the play) of what really happened — this IS the play
> - **Catharsis**: The audience processes the grief of gender-based injustice, the pain of silenced truth

> [!TIP] Stage Design as Metaphor
> The multi-level stage (Dr. Thakkar elevated, Dan's bedsitter realistic, Patel house submerged in memory) mirrors the **power hierarchy** in Tara:
> - Elevated = authority (medical, masculine, institutional)
> - Ground level = reality (Dan grappling with truth NOW)
> - Lower/memory = where violence happened + was buried
> Compare with [[STUDY GUIDE - Introduction to Literary Studies#SECTION 3: TARA by Mahesh Dattani]] for detailed stage design analysis.

## Bharti's Disability
- She literally cannot speak the truth about what happened in the hospital.
- She knew the truth but was rendered powerless by patriarchal structures
- Disabled motherhood, can't protect, or be honest with her daughter.
- She is internally just as disabled as Tara.

> [!TIP] Compare: Bharti's Silence
> Bharti's inability to speak parallels the "absent voice" of Kannaki in [[The Book of Vanci]] — both women are gagged by the systems they exist in. Also compare with how Draupadi's questions in [[Mahabharata]] are dismissed by the male court.

## Patel's Disability
- His inability to see women's as equal as son's.
- His inability to take responsibility: He cannot confess, apologize, or face consequences.
- Emotionally disabled in a way, He Cannot express love, remorse, or genuine connection with his children. 
- He's a father who actively harmed his child.

> [!NOTE] Patel vs. Creon
> Compare Patel with [[Antigone - Sophocles|Creon]] — both are patriarchal authority figures whose rigid gender assumptions destroy the people they are supposed to protect. Creon loses Antigone; Patel loses Tara.

## Tara's Disability
- Mutilated body, only one leg when born. 
- Her body was deliberately sacrificed, in hopes her brother would have a better life.
- Disabled agency: she was robbed of her own story, never got to know the truth about her body.
- Disabled future, 

## Dan's Disability
- At first, he received 'better' body part, which later becomes the source of his trauma.
- Unable to forgive himself or his parents 
- His entire identity is built on a violence, he didn't even agree to in the first place.
- He is running away from his past, his story.

> [!TIP] Dan Writing the Play = Anagnorisis
> Dan's act of writing the play is his [[3 Tragic Beats|Anagnorisis]] — the critical discovery. He cannot escape his guilt without confronting it through narrative. This parallels [[Doctor Faustus - Christopher Marlowe|Faustus's]] final monologue — both characters face the full weight of what they've done too late to change it.

## Society's Disability
- Dr. Thakkar can be bribed; medical decisions based on gender, not medical need 
- No accountability for what is essentially gender-based violence
- Language Disability: what word would you use to describe what happened to Tara? Assault?  Medical malpractice.
