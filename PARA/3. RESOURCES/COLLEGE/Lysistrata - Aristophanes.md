# 🔐 LYSISTRATA — THE 6 CONTENT BANKS (DU EXAM MASTER SET)

---

## 1️⃣ PLOT EPISODES BANK

_(What happens + why it matters)_

### (a) Opening Assembly of Women

- Lysistrata gathers women from different Greek city-states (Athens, Sparta, Corinth).
    
- Shows **female solidarity beyond political borders**.
    
- Women initially resist → highlights internalised patriarchy.
    
- Establishes Lysistrata as a **leader and organiser**.
    

👉 Use this to discuss **leadership, unity, political awakening**.

---

### (b) The Sex Strike

- Women refuse sexual relations until men agree to peace.
    
- Domestic power is converted into **political leverage**.
    
- Men's inability to control desire exposes masculine weakness.
    

👉 Use this for **gender reversal, body politics, anti-war themes**.

---

### (c) Occupation of the Acropolis

- Women seize the treasury.
    
- Control of **state finances** = control of war machinery.
    
- Symbolic takeover of public space by women.
    

👉 Very strong for **female agency + political satire**.

---

### (d) Magistrate Scene

- Magistrate enters with police and crowbars.
    
- Represents **coercive male authority**.
    
- Lysistrata defeats him with reason and ridicule.
    
- Women dress him as a woman → humiliation of power.
    

👉 Use for **patriarchy, misogyny, satire of authority**.

---

### (e) Adonis Lament Reference

- Woman mourning Adonis while war planning continues.
    
- Ignored warning → later proven prophetic.
    
- Women understand **cost of war**, men ignore it.
    

👉 Use for **irony, foresight of women, anti-war critique**.

---

### (f) Sexual Frustration Scenes

- Men suffer physically from desire.
    
- Comic exaggeration exposes war as **unnatural and self-destructive**.
    

👉 Use for **obscenity as political tool**.

---

### (g) Final Peace Negotiation

- Athens and Sparta reconcile.
    
- Lysistrata mediates.
    
- Body of a woman used as map of Greece.
    

👉 Shows:

- Absurdity of war
    
- Restoration of order
    
- Limits of feminist victory
    

---

## 2️⃣ THEMES BANK

_(Abstract ideas — always expandable)_

### (a) Anti-War Ideology

- War as:
    
    - Irrational
        
    - Wasteful
        
    - Male obsession
        
- Focus on domestic suffering, not battlefield glory.
    

---

### (b) Gender and Power

- Reversal of gender roles:
    
    - Women = rational
        
    - Men = emotional
        
- Patriarchal fear of female agency.
    
> [!NOTE] Compare with Other Texts
> - [[Mahabharata|Draupadi's question in the dice game]] — women challenging male authority within patriarchal constraints. See [[Draupadi is often seen as 'Nathabati Anathabath' or 'Five lords', Yet none a 'Protector'. Discuss]]
> - [[TARA play|Tara]] — patriarchy physically harms Tara while rendering Bharati unable to speak the truth
> - [[PRIDE AND PREJUDICE|Elizabeth Bennet]] — a woman asserting rational agency against social convention

---

### (c) Female Solidarity

- Women unite across cities.
    
- Shared suffering transcends politics.
    
> [!TIP] Cross-text connection
> Compare with how women in [[Antigone - Sophocles|Antigone]] (Ismene's initial refusal vs Antigone's resolve) shows a more complicated picture of female solidarity under patriarchy.

---

### (d) Sexual Politics

- Body as power
    
- Desire as weakness
    
- Control over intimacy = control over state
    

---

### (e) Public vs Private Sphere

- Domestic space becomes political.
    
- Challenges Athenian gender norms.
    

---

### (f) Reason vs Force

- Lysistrata uses dialogue.
    
- Magistrate uses violence.
    
- Reason wins.
    

---

## 3️⃣ CHARACTERS BANK

_(Always analyse in contrast)_

### Lysistrata

- Rational
    
- Strategic
    
- Calm
    
- Moral authority
    
- Political intelligence
    

👉 Contrast with male leaders.
👉 Compare with [[PRIDE AND PREJUDICE|Elizabeth Bennet]] — both women are rational, witty, and subversive within patriarchal systems.

---

### Magistrate

- Blind authority
    
- Misogynistic
    
- Relies on force
    
- Represents failed politics
    

---

### Female Chorus

- Collective voice
    
- Defenders of peace
    
- Organised resistance
    

---

### Male Chorus

- Aggressive
    
- Fearful of loss of control
    
- Represent war mentality
    

---

### Myrrhine (key example)

- Personalises sex strike
    
- Manipulates male desire
    
- Shows intelligence through action
    

---

## 4️⃣ CONTEXT BANK

_(Marks live here)_

### Historical Context

- Peloponnesian War (431–404 BCE)
    
- Sicilian Expedition disaster
    
- War fatigue
    

---

### Social Context

- Women excluded from politics
    
- Men dominated public life
    
- Aristophanes challenges this
    
> [!NOTE] Aristophanes & Comedy as Political Speech
> Comedy allowed criticism that tragedy could not. See [[Aristotalian Idea of Drama]] and [[Aristotle's Poetics]] on the role of comedy (the lost half of Poetics).
> Similarly, [[A Modest Proposal - Johnathan Swift|Jonathan Swift's "A Modest Proposal"]] uses dark [[Juvenalian Satire|Juvenalian satire]] to make political critiques that direct speech could not — see [[Satire in Literature]].

---

### Performance Context

- Performed at Athenian festivals
    
- Male audience
    
- Comedy allowed criticism
    

---

## 5️⃣ LITERARY DEVICES BANK

_(Explain function, not just name)_

### Satire

- Ridicules war and authority
    
- See [[Satire in Literature]] for broader theory
- Compare with [[A Modest Proposal - Johnathan Swift|A Modest Proposal]] — both use satire to expose political failures

---

### Irony

- Women dismissed but proven right
- See [[Aristotalian Idea of Drama]] on dramatic irony    

---

### Obscenity

- Undermines masculine seriousness
    
- Exposes hypocrisy
    

---

### Exaggeration

- Physical comedy
    
- Sexual suffering
    

---

### Chorus

- Commentary
    
- Represents social groups
    
- See [[Aristotle's Poetics]] — the role of Chorus in Greek drama

---

### Symbolism

- Acropolis = power
    
- Female body = Greece
    
- Sex strike = denial of war fuel
    

---

## 6️⃣ CRITICISM / INTERPRETATION BANK

_(This is how answers sound mature)_

### Feminist Reading

- Women as political agents
    
- Temporary empowerment
    
- Patriarchy restored
    

---

### Anti-War Reading

- Critique of militarism
    
- Exposure of political stupidity
    
> [!TIP] Parallel anti-war texts
> The Mahabharata also critiques the heroic ideal — see [[In what ways does the Dicing episode provide a critique of the heroic ideal in the Mahabharata]]

---

### Comic Strategy

- Comedy as safe political speech
    
- Laughter as resistance
    

---

### Limitations

- Women's power conditional
    
- Peace achieved through sexuality
    
- Reinforces some stereotypes
