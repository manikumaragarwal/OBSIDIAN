# Macbeth — Historical & Theatrical Context

> [!INFO] Understanding Context for Exams
> Macbeth was written in 1606, during the reign of **James I**. Understanding this context is crucial for answering exam questions about WHY Shakespeare wrote the the way he did.

---

## The Historical Timeline

| Year | Event | Connection to Macbeth |
|------|-------|----------------------|
| **1567** | Historical Macbeth defeated; James I (James VI of Scotland) born 500+ years later | Historical distance allows Shakespeare to fictionalize |
| **1603** | **James I** becomes King of England → James VI of Scotland becomes England's monarch | **Crucial** — Macbeth is about Scottish kings for a Scottish-English king |
| **1605** | **Gunpowder Plot** — Catholic conspirators try to blow up Parliament; execution of Guy Fawkes | **Immediate context** — Play explores regicide (killing king), treason |
| **1606** | **Macbeth** likely first performed | Written with Gunpowder Plot trauma fresh in English mind |

---

## King James I — The Play's Intended Audience

### Why James I Matters

| James's Obsession | Connection to Macbeth |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Witchcraft** | He wrote *Daemonologie*; personally oversaw witch trials | Witches are prominent, their prophecy drives tragedy |
| **Scottish Heritage** | Born James VI of Scotland | Play set in Scotland, uses Scottish history |
| **Banquo's Lineage** | James claimed descent from Banquo | Shakespeare **changed history** to make Banquo virtuous (his descendants = the Stuarts) |

> [!TIP] Royal Flattery
> Macbeth is **NOT historically accurate**:
- Historical Banquo was ALMOST CERTAINLY involved in the murder
- Shakespeare made Banquo **virtuous** because James claimed descent from him
- This is **political flattery** — making the current king's ancestor a hero

### James I's Views on Kingship

James believed strongly in:
- **Divine Right of Kings** — Kings are appointed by God, answer only to God
- **Regicide is the ultimate crime** — Killing a king = killing God's representative
- **Treason deserves punishment by hell** — Any disloyalty deserves divine wrath

Macbeth explores these ideas through:
- Duncan's murder disrupting natural order
- Nature going haywig after Duncan's death
- Macbeth's ultimate damnation

## The Gunpowder Plot (1605) — Immediate Context

### What Happened

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **When** | November 5, 1605 |
| **Who** | Catholic conspirators led by Guy Fawkes |
| **What** | Tried to kill King James I by blowing up Parliament |
| **Outcome** | Plot discovered, conspirators executed |

**Immediate psychological impact**: The English were **traumatized** by being SO CLOSE to their king's murder.

---

### How Gunpowder Plot Shapes Macbeth

| Element in Macbeth | Connection to Gunpowder Plot |
|--------------------|------------------------------|
| **Duncan's murder** | Mirrors the PLOT against James I (even if not carried out) |
| **Treason language** | "Bloody instructions" — echoes talk of Catholic treason |
| **Regicide as ultimate sin** | Reinforces what everyone felt: Killing king = cosmic horror |
| **Torture and execution images** | Reflects REAL punishments of plotters |
| **Suspicion and paranoia** | Macbeth's court resembles real court after plot |

> [!TIP] For Exam Essays | When asked about historical context, mention **both** James I AND the Gunpowder Plot. Both contributed to the play's themes.

## Witchcraft in Jacobean England

### King James's Role in Witchcraft History

| James's Action | Significance |
|----------------|--------------|
| **Wrote *Daemonologie* (1597)** | Theology book arguing witches were real and dangerous |
| **Personally oversaw witch trials** | Not just policy — actively involved in prosecutions |
| **Believed in the supernatural** | Saw witches as tools of Satan |

### The Witches in Macbeth

 witches in the play reflect **Jacobean anxieties**:

| Witch Characteristic | Jacobean Context |
|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Prophecy** | King James feared prophecy but believed in it |
| **Supernatural power** | Belief that witches could manipulate fate |
| **Moral ambiguity** | Jacobeans believed witches were evil but people weren't sure |

> [!NOTE] Feminist Reading
| The witches are **complex**:
> - They're marginalized (three women)
> - They have power (more than men seem to)
> - They're **not simply evil** — they're agents of chaotic fate
> - This reflects BOTH fear AND fascination with women's power

## The Real Macbeth — Historical Truth vs. Shakespeare

| Aspect | Historical Macbeth | Shakespeare's Macbeth |
|--------|--------------------|---------------------|
| **Ruled** | 1040–1057 | Murdered shortly after taking power |
| **Length of reign** | 17 successful years | Brief, chaotic |
| **Character** | Generally seen as competent ruler | Tyrannical murderer |
| **Banquo** | Possibly involved in Duncan's murder | Virtuous ancestor of kings |
| **Fate** | Died in battle against Malcolm | Killed by Macduff |

---

### Why Shakespeare Changed History

| Reason | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| **Royal flattery** | Make Banquo virtuous → flatter James I |
| **Tragedy structure** | Need a fall from greatness, not a history lesson |
| **Moral warning** | Show regicide's consequences → reinforce divine right |
| **Dramatic tension** | Historical details would slow the play |

> [!TIP] For Exam Questions | When asked about history vs. fiction:
> 1. **Acknowledge** Shakespeare changed history
> 2. **Explain** WHY (political reasons, dramatic needs)
> 3. **Focus** on what the **fiction** reveals about themes

## Great Chain of Being — Elizabethan Worldview

The Elizabethans and Jacobeans believed in the **Great Chain of Being**:

```
God
 ↓
Angels
 ↓
Humans (Kings → Nobles → Commoners → Peasants)
 ↓
Animals (Noble animals → Common animals)
 ↓
Plants
 ↓
Minerals
```

### How This Shapes Macbeth

| Disruption | Example | Effect |
|------------|---------|--------|
| **Killing God's king** | Duncan's murder | Breaks chain → ALL of nature disrupts |
| **Animals act unnaturally** | Owl kills falcon, horses eat each other | Shows chain broken |
| **Order restored** | Malcolm becomes king | Chain re-established |

> [!NOTE] Why Earthquakes After Duncan's Death:
> "The earth was feverous and did shake."

| Meaning in Chain of Being |
|--------------------------|
| The **foundation** (earth) is shaken because the **top** (king) is removed |
| Chaos spreads from top to bottom through the entire cosmic order |
| Killing a king is not just political — it's a **cosmic sacrilege** |

## Gender and Class in Jacobean England

### Gender Roles

| Expectation | Shown in Macbeth |
|-------------|------------------|
| **Men** should be warriors, decisive | Macbeth becomes a warrior-king |
| **Men** should NOT be weak | Told "When you durst do it, then you were a man" |
| **Women** should be nurturing | Lady Macbeth must "unsex" herself to have power |

### Class Hierarchy

| Level | Shown in Macbeth |
|-------|------------------|
| **Kings** → divine right | Duncan's murder disrupts cosmic order |
| **Thanes** → loyalty to king | Macbeth's betrayal is treachery |
| **Witches** → outside normal hierarchy | They exist beyond human social structure |

## Theatrical Context — Globe Theatre

| Element | Connection to Macbeth |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Globe Theatre** | Open-air, daylight performances → No artificial lighting → **"Stars, hide your fires"** is dramatic, not literal |

| **All-male casts**Lady Macbeth played by boy actor → Adds layer to "unsex me here"
| **Audience diversity** | Both educated and uneducated → Need both high themes and base comedy (Porter) |
