# Macbeth — Important Quotes for Exams

> [!INFO] Quote Analysis Strategy
> For exam essays, always analyze quotes FOUR ways:
> 1. **Context:** Who's speaking, when, and what just happened?
> 2. **Imagery:** Visual, sensory details (blood, darkness, nature)
> 3. **Themes:** Which major themes does this connect to?
> 4. **Character:** What does this reveal about the speaker?

---

## Opening Lines — Setting the Tone

### "Fair is foul, and foul is fair."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Weird Sisters (1.1) |
| **Context** | Opening line of the play, right after thunder and lightning |
| **Paradox** | Good is evil, evil is good — the world is morally inverted |
| **Themes** | Appearance vs. Reality, Fate vs. Free Will, Moral Disorder |
| **Symbolism** | Establishes the **chaotic atmosphere** where "nothing is what it seems" |

> [!TIP] Exam Use
> This is a **perfect opening quote** for essays on:
> - Appearance vs. Reality
> - The play's moral universe
> - How the witches manipulate Macbeth

## The Prophecy — Catalyst for Tragedy

### "All hail, Macbeth! hail to thee, Thane of Glamis! / All hail, Macbeth! hail to thee, Thane of Cawdor! / All hail, Macbeth, that shalt be king hereafter!"

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | First Witch (1.3) |
| **Context** | Macbeth and Banquo encounter the witches after the battle |
| **Structure** | Three prophecies: past truth, present truth, future possibility |
| **Significance** | **First [[Peripeteia]]** — sudden reversal of fortune; plants the seed of ambition |

### "Thou shalt get kings, though thou be none."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Third Witch to Banquo (1.3) |
| **Context** | The witches give a contrasting prophecy to Banquo |
| **Significance** | **Crucial for Macbeth's jealousy** — his line will NOT succeed, Banquo's WILL |
| **Themes** | Ambition, Jealousy, Fate vs. Free Will |

> [!NOTE] Why This Matters
> This creates **psychological pressure** on Macbeth that drives the murder of Banquo. Without this, Macbeth's ambition might have been satisfied with just becoming king.

## Lady Macbeth's Introduction

### "Yet do I fear thy nature; / It is too full o' the milk of human kindness."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Lady Macbeth, reading Macbeth's letter (1.5) |
| **Context** | She's analyzing her husband's character before they even speak |
| **Imagery** | "Milk" = innocence, tenderness, femininity |
| **Significance** | Shows she **understands** Macbeth's flaw — he's ambitious but has a conscience |

### "Come, you spirits / That tend on mortal thoughts, unsex me here."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Lady Macbeth (1.5) |
| **Context** | Praying to lose her femininity before Macbeth arrives |
| **Meaning** | "Unsex me" = remove my feminine nature (tenderness, "milk") |
| **Significance** | Shows her belief that **power requires rejection of femininity** |

## Macbeth's Moral Struggle

### "I have no spur / To prick the sides of my intent, but only / Vaulting ambition, which o'erleaps itself / And falls on the other."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Macbeth, soliloquy (1.7) |
| **Context** | Wrestling with whether to kill Duncan |
| **Imagery** | "Prick the sides" (like a horse), "vaulting" (jumps over) |
| **Meaning** | Ambition is like a horse that JUMPS TOO HIGH and falls |
| **Significance** | Macbeth's **[[Hamartia|tragic flaw]]** — he KNOWS it's a problem but can't stop |

### "When you durst do it, then you were a man."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Lady Macbeth to Macbeth (1.7) |
| **Context** | Persuading him to commit the murder |
| **Themes** | Masculinity, Gender, Power |
| **Significance** | Equates **manhood with violence** — a toxic masculinity trap |

### "I have given suck, and know / How tender 'tis to love the babe that milks me."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Lady Macbeth (1.7) |
| **Context** | Using maternal imagery to manipulate Macbeth |
| **Significance** | THE MOST MYSTERIOUS LINE — suggests she **understands loss** deeply |
| **Debate** | Does this mean she killed a baby? Most critics say NO — it's *rhetorical* |

## The Murder & Its Aftermath

### "Is this a dagger which I see before me, / The handle toward my hand?"

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Macbeth, soliloquy (2.1) |
| **Context** | About to kill Duncan; sees hallucinated dagger |
| **Themes** | Guilt, Hallucination, Moral Decay |
| **Significance** | **First sign of psychological breakdown** — guilt taking physical form |

### "Macbeth does murder sleep."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Macbeth (2.2) |
| **Context** | Immediately after killing Duncan |
| **Meaning** | By killing Duncan, he's killed **innocence, peace, rest**
| **Significance** | Sleep in Shakespeare = **divine peace for the worthy**; now gone forever |

### "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood / Clean from my hand?"

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Macbeth (2.2) |
| **Context** | Looking at his blood-covered hands |
| **Imagery** | Neptune = Roman god of the sea; "all the ocean" |
| **Meaning** | Can even GOD'S water cleanse him? Answer: **NO** |
| **Themes** | Guilt, Inescapable Sin, Divine Judgment |

### "A little water clears us of this deed."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Lady Macbeth (2.2) |
| **Context** | Telling Macbeth they can wash away their guilt |
| **Themes** | Denial, Guilt, Appearance vs. Reality |
| **Significance** | Shows her **COMPLETE denial** of the moral weight |
| **Irony** | Later: "Out, damned spot! ... All the perfumes of Arabia..." |

|---------|----------|

| **Context** | Seeing imaginary blood on her hands |
| **Themes** | Guilt, Conscience, Inescapable Sin |
| **Irony** | She once said "a little water clears us" — now NOTHING cleans her |

### "What's done cannot be undone."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Lady Macbeth, sleep-walking (5.1) |
| **Context** | Final recognition of their crime's permanence |
| **Significance** | Only NOW does she understand what she denied from the start |

### "All the perfumes of Arabia will not sweeten this little hand."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|

| **Imagery** | "All the perfumes of Arabia" = even the finest, most expensive fragrances |

## Existential Despair

### "She should have died hereafter; / There would have been a time for such a word."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Macbeth, hearing of Lady Macbeth's death (5.5) |
| **Context** | Ambiguous response to her death |
| **Debated meaning** | 1) Stunned grief OR 2) Nihilism — life is meaningless anyway |

### "Life's but a walking shadow, a poor player / That struts and frets his hour upon the stage / And then is heard no more."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Macbeth, "Tomorrow" soliloquy (5.5) |
| **Imagery** | "Walking shadow" = no substance, "poor player" = just acting, "struts and frets" = meaningless motion |
| **Themes** | Time, Meaninglessness, Death, Ambition's Futility |
| **Significance** | Macbeth's **[[Anagnorisis]]** — realizes ambition was WORTHLESS |

### "A tale / Told by an idiot, full of sound and fury, / Signifying nothing."

| Element | Analysis |
|---------|----------|
| **Speaker** | Macbeth, "Tomorrow" soliloquy (5.5) |
| **Meaning** | Life = story without meaning, noise without substance |
| **Themes** | Nihilism, Existential Despair, Ambition's Hollowness |

# Quote Analysis — Essay Questions

When asked to analyze a quote, structure your answer like this:

1. **Identify** the speaker, context, and scene
2. **Explain** the literal meaning
3. **Analyze** the imagery and literary devices
4. **Connect** to themes (ambition, guilt, fate, etc.)
5. **Show character development** — what this reveals about the speaker
6. **Link** to the larger tragedy — how this moves the play toward its end
