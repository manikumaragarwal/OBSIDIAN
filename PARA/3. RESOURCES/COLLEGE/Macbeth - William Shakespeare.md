# Macbeth - William Shakespeare (1606)

> [!SUMMARY] Macbeth at a Glance
> Written during **James I's reign** (1603-1625), Macbeth is Shakespeare's shortest tragedy exploring how **unchecked ambition** transforms a noble warrior into a murderous tyrant. The play is steeped in Scottish history, witchcraft beliefs, and the political anxieties of its time.

## Historical & Theatrical Context

### Why This Play Became a Masterpiece
- **Written c. 1606**: Early Jacobean era, soon after the **Gunpowder Plot (1605)** when Catholics tried to blow up Parliament
- **Royal Flattery**: King James I was OBSESSED with:
  - **Witchcraft** → He wrote *Daemonologie* and personally oversaw witch trials
  - **Scottish heritage** → He was James VI of Scotland before becoming England's king
  - **Banquo's lineage** → James claimed descent from Banquo (Shakespeare changed history to flatter him)

### The Real Macbeth
- **Historical Macbeth (Mac Bethad mac Findláich)**: Ruled Scotland from 1040-1057
- Actually a SUCCESSFUL king who ruled for 17 years
- **Shakespeare's twist**: Transformed him into a villain, likely to:
  - Flatter the Stuart monarchs (who descended from Malcolm III)
  - Create a warning about the **dangers of regicide** (killing a king)

# Plot Summary — By Act

## Act One: The Seeds of Ambition

### Scene 1: The Weird Sisters
| Element                          | Significance                                                            |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Fair is foul, and foul is fair" | Sets the play's theme of **moral inversion** - nothing is what it seems |
| Paradoxical language             | Witches exist in a liminal space between reality and prophecy           |
| Thunder & lightning              | Establishes **chaotic atmosphere**, setting the tone                    |


### Scene 2: The Noble Warrior
> "Brave Macbeth—well he deserves that name..."

Before meeting the witches, Macbeth is described as:
- "Bellona's bridegroom" (husband of war goddess)
- "Disdaining fortune" (not relying on luck, but skill)
- Carves his way through battle "from the nave to the chops" (from belly to jaw)

**Key Insight**: This establishes Macbeth's **hamartia (tragic flaw)** — he already has the capacity for violence. The witches don't create evil; they reveal what's already latent.


### Scene 3: The Prophecy (First [[Peripeteia|Reversal]])

> "All hail, Macbeth! hail to thee, Thane of Glamis! / All hail, Macbeth! hail to thee, Thane of Cawdor! / All hail, Macbeth, that shalt be king hereafter!"

The three prophecies to Macbeth:
1. **Thane of Glamis** — already true (he holds this title)
2. **Thane of Cawdor** — becomes true moments later
3. **King hereafter** — the seed of his destruction

The prophecy to Banquo:
> "Thou shalt get kings, though thou be none."

This is CRUCIAL. It tells Macbeth:
- His line will NOT succeed him
- His friend Banquo's descendants WILL be kings
- This creates **jealousy and paranoia** that drives later murders


### Scene 5: Lady Macbeth — The Catalyst
> "Yet do I fear thy nature; / It is too full o' the milk of human kindness"

Lady Macbeth reads her husband's letter and immediately:
1. **Analyses Macbeth's character** — he's ambitious but has a conscience
2. **Prays to be "unsexed"** — wants her feminine "milk" replaced with "gall" (bile/bitterness)
3. **Invokes darkness** — "Come, thick night, and pall thee in the dunnest smoke of hell"

**Critical Reading**: Lady Macbeth is NOT simply "evil." She's a woman in a patriarchal society who must summon supernatural strength to achieve what comes naturally to ambitious men.


### Scene 7: The Decision
Macbeth's famous soliloquy — wrestling with his conscience:

> "If it were done when 'tis done, then 'twere well / It were done quickly."

His arguments AGAINST killing Duncan:
1. Duncan is his **kinsman and cousin**
2. Duncan is his **host** (sacred duty of hospitality)
3. Duncan has been a **good ruler**
4. Duncan's **virtues** will "plead like angels, trumpet-tongued, against / The deep damnation of his taking-off"

Lady Macbeth's persuasion:
- Calls him a **coward** ("When you durst do it, then you were a man")
- Brings up their **child**: "I have given suck, and know / How tender 'tis to love the babe that milks me"
- **Twist**: She implies she would kill her own baby to keep an oath
- **Reality check**: This is likely BLUSTER, not literal — Shakespeare showing us the **psychological toll** of their ambition

## Act Two: The Murder Unfolds

### The Dagger Soliloquy
> "Is this a dagger which I see before me, / The handle toward my hand?"

**Analysis**: This is the FIRST sign of Macbeth's **psychological deterioration**. The dagger represents:
- **Guilt manifesting as hallucination**
- The **irreversible path** he's choosing
- **Blood** that "will have blood" (recurs throughout the play)


### The Murder — Intimate Horror
Unlike what we might expect, Shakespeare CUTS the murder scene. We don't watch it. Why?

**Theatrical genius**: By NOT showing the murder, he makes it:
- **More terrifying** — our imagination fills in worse than any staging
- **More intimate** — the horror is in Macbeth's mind
- **More tragic** — we focus on the MAN, not just the violence


### Lady Macbeth's Drugged Grooms
> "That which hath made them drunk, hath made me bold"

She uses the guards' drunkenness to:
1. Frame them for the murder
2. Provide the "witnesses" that were never actually there
3. Plant the daggers to make it look like they did it

**Irony**: Lady Macbeth frames two drunk men when SHE has been "made drunk" by ambition and the spirit she invoked.


### Post-Murder: "A little water clears us of this deed"

Macbeth returns haunted:
> "Macbeth does murder sleep"

This line reveals what he's most afraid of — losing his peace of mind. Sleep in Shakespeare = innocence, rest, the soul's time to heal. By murdering sleep, Macbeth ensures **eternal insomnia and guilt**.

Lady Macbeth dismisses his horror, telling him to wash up. This shows:
- She's in **denial** about the reality
- She believes the physical act is all that matters
- She doesn't understand the **psychological toll** that awaits them


---


## Act Three: Tyranny Takes Root

### The Banquet Scene — Psychological Horror

The **turning point** of the play. Macbeth is now king, but paranoid.

| Event | Significance |
|-------|-------------|
| Murder of Banquo | Eliminates the one who threatened his children's succession |
| Banquo's ghost appears | ONLY Macbeth can see it — guilt-made visible |
| Lady Macbeth must excuse her husband | Their roles REVERSED — she's now the "strong" one |


> "Thou canst not say I did it. Never shake / Thy gory locks at me."

This is **[[Peripeteia|peripeteia]]** — Macbeth's kinghood has reversed into madness. The more power he gets, the less control he has over himself.


### "Blood will have blood"
The cycle of violence escalates. Macbeth hires murderers to kill:
1. Banquo
2. Fleance (who escapes, fulfilling the prophecy)

This creates a **self-fulfilling prophecy**: Macbeth's attempts to prevent his fate CAUSE his fate.


---

## Act Four: More Murder, More Madness

### The Apparitions — False Security
When the witches show Macbeth three apparitions, he's given **seeming comfort**:

| Apparition | Prophecy | Reality |
|------------|----------|---------|
| Armed Head | "Beware Macduff" | **True** — Macduff will kill him |
| Bloody Child | "None of woman born shall harm Macbeth" | **Wordplay** — Macduff was "from his mother's womb untimely ripped" (C-section) |
| Child Crowned with Tree | "Great Birnam Wood to high Dunsinane Hill shall come" | **Literal truth** — soldiers carry branches as camouflage |


> "Give me a smooth answer."

Macbeth demands prophecies that comfort him. This shows his **hubris** — he only hears what he wants to hear.

### The Macduff Massacre — Pointless Cruelty
> "From this moment / The very firstlings of my heart shall be / The firstlings of my hand."

Macbeth decides: NO MORE hesitation. He will act immediately on his impulses.

**Result**: He sends murderers to:
1. Kill Macduff's castle
2. Murder Macduff's wife
3. Murder Macduff's children

This murder is DIFFERENT from Duncan's killing:
- Duncan = political ambition + personal ambition
- Macduff's family = PURE cruelty, no strategic benefit
- This is the moment Macbeth becomes a **monster**, not just an ambitious man


### Lady Macbeth's Sleep-Walking Scene
> "Out, damned spot! out, I say!"

This is the **[[Anagnorisis|recognition]]** moment that the previous Macbeth had. Now Lady Macbeth is haunted by:
- **"Yet here's a spot"** — she's seeing blood on her hands
- **"What's done cannot be undone"** — finally understanding the weight of their actions
- **"The smell of the blood... all the perfumes of Arabia will not sweeten"** — guilt that cannot be washed away

**Tragic Realization**: The woman who once said "a little water clears us of this deed" now cannot be cleansed even by **all the perfumes of Arabia**. The **denial has collapsed**.


---

## Act Five: Downfall

### "Out, out, brief candle"
Lady Macbeth dies offstage. We don't see it, only hear it.

> "She should have died hereafter; / There would have been a time for such a word."

Macbeth's response is famously **ambiguous**:
- **Interpretation 1**: He's in shock — stunned grief
- **Interpretation 2**: He's nihilistic — life is meaningless anyway
> Life is...
> "...but a walking shadow, a poor player / That struts and frets his hour upon the stage / And then is heard no more."
> "A tale / Told by an idiot, full of sound and fury, / Signifying nothing."
This is **existential despair**. Macbeth has reached complete spiritual emptiness.


### The Final Battle — Fulfillment of Prophecies
| Prophecy | Fulfillment | Meaning |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| Beware Macduff | Macduff is the one who kills him | True |
| None of woman born | Macduff was C-section born | Word-games cannot save you from fate |
| Birnam Wood moves | Soldiers carry branches as camouflage | Literally fulfilled |

**Tragic Irony**: Macbeth spent the entire play trying to CONTROL his destiny, only to have his own efforts **CREATE the very thing he feared**.


### Macbeth's End
> "Lay on, Macduff, / And damn'd be him that first cried, 'Hold, enough!'"

Unlike Faustus (who begs for mercy), Macbeth chooses:
- **Dignified resistance**
- Acceptance of his inevitable fate
- A final moment of **honesty** — no more pretending

# Character Analysis

## Macbeth — The Tragic Hero

| Trait | Evidence | Significance |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| **Noble beginning** | "Brave Macbeth," "Bellona's bridegroom" | Establishes him as [[Aristotle's Poetics\|tragic hero]] of high status |
| **[[Hamartia|Tragic flaw]]** | Ambition + suggestibility | His fatal weakness |
| **[[Peripeteia|Reversal]]** | Murder of Duncan | The moment he becomes a tyrant |
| **[[Anagnorisis|Recognition]]** | Brief candle soliloquy | Realizes the emptiness of his ambition |

> [!NOTE] Macbeth vs. Other Tragic Heroes
> - **Unlike Oedipus**: Macbeth CHOOSES evil where Oedipus was **born into tragedy**
> - **Unlike Hamlet**: Macbeth ACTS immediately where Hamlet DELAYS; both lead to tragedy
> - **Like Faustus**: Macbeth sells his soul for power, but with **more introspection** along the way


## Lady Macbeth — The Complex Anti-Heroine

| Attribute | Evidence | Analysis |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Ambitious** | "Come, you spirits that tend on mortal thoughts" | Shares Macbeth's hunger for power |
| **Ruthless** | "I have given suck... I would dash the brains out" | Willing to use extreme imagery to achieve goals |
| **Psychologically fragile** | Sleep-walking, "out damned spot" | Her guilt manifests as insanity |
| **Tragic** | Dies offstage | denied the redemptive death Macbeth gets |


> [!TIP] Feminist Readings
> Lady Macbeth is often read as a critique of **female ambition** in a patriarchal society. To achieve what comes naturally to men (power), she must **reject her femininity**, and when the violence becomes too much, she **breaks**. This mirrors how society punishes women who transgress gender norms.


> [!NOTE] Compare with Other Female Characters
> - **Antigone** ([[Antigone - Sophocles|Antigone]]): Choses MORAL law over law; Lady Macbeth chooses POWER over morality
> - **Draupadi** ([[Mahabharata]]): Both are wronged by the system; Draupadi fights it with DIALOGUE, Lady Macbeth with VIOLENCE


## The Weird Sisters — Fate or Catalyst?

The witches' role remains debated:

| Interpretation | Evidence | Problems |
|----------------|----------|----------|
| **They control fate** | They speak prophecies that come true | Contradicts Shakespeare's emphasis on **free will** |
| **They merely reveal truth** | Macbeth already has ambition | Doesn't explain their active role in temptation |
| **They're psychological projections** | Only Macbeth and Banquo see them | Doesn't explain their otherworldly nature |


> [!IMPORTANT] Critical Consensus
> Shakespeare presents them as **ambiguous** — they exist at the intersection of **external fate** (prophecy) and **internal desire** (ambition). This reflects the **Renaissance debate**: Do we control our destiny, or is it written in the stars?


## Macduff — The Avenger

| Trait | Significance |
|-------|-------------|
| "None of woman born" | Represents **exception to rules** |
| Lost his wife & children | His revenge is **pure, not political** |
| "Not for their own demerits, but for mine" | Takes on the burden of justice|

Macduff is the **moral foil** to Macbeth:
- Both lost family members (Macbeth's children are never born)
- Both act on revenge
- Macduff's revenge is JUSTIFIED, Macbeth's is NOT

# Themes & Symbols

## Ambition — The Core Tragedy

> "I have no spur / To prick the sides of my intent, but only / Vaulting ambition, which o'erleaps itself."

Ambition in Macbeth:
- **Noble**: Can drive greatness (Macbeth's battlefield bravery)
- **Corrupting**: When unchecked, transforms the soul
- **Inescapable**: Once started, it creates an **addiction cycle**

> [!NOTE] Ambition Across Texts
> - **Mahabharata** ([[Mahabharata]]): Duryodhana's ambition for the throne leads to war
> - **Faustus** ([[Doctor Faustus - Christopher Marlowe]]): Ambition for omniscience leads to damnation
> - **Macbeth**: Ambition for power leads to tyrannicide and death


## Appearance vs. Reality

The play's central paradox: **"Fair is foul, and foul is fair"**

| Appearance | Reality | Scene |
|------------|---------|-------|
| Duncan is a "gracious" king | His murder is necessary for Macbeth's ambition | Act 1, Scene 7 |
| Lady Macbeth is "unsexed" and strong | She's psychologically fragile | Sleep-walking scene |
| Macbeth is "secure" from threats | He's most vulnerable to himself | After banquet scene |
| Prophecies seem to protect him | They actually trap him | Apparitions scene |


## Guilt & Conscience

Guilt manifests PHYSICALLY and PSYCHOLOGICALLY:

| Character | Guilt Forms |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Macbeth** | Hallucinations, insomnia, paranoia |
| **Lady Macbeth** | Sleep-walking, imaginary blood, obsessive washing |


> [!NOTE] Guilt as Dramatic Device
> Shakespeare shows us that **conscience cannot be murdered**. When you violate your deepest moral sense, it becomes a **tortured presence** in your mind. This makes Macbeth a **psychological study** as much as a political tragedy.


## Order vs. Chaos

| Element | Represents | Evidence |
|---------|------------|----------|
| **King** | Natural order, divine right | Duncan's rule brings fertility and prosperity |
| **Macbeth** | Unnatural disorder | Weather changes, animals behave strangely after Duncan's murder |
| **Macduff** | Restoration of order | He kills Macbeth and puts Malcolm on throne |


> "The earth was feverous and did shake."
> "Tis unnatural, / Even like the deed that's done."

After Duncan's murder, NATURE goes haywig. Animals behave strangely, the night won't fall, and an owl kills a falcon. This is **Shakespearean symbolism**: violating natural law (killing a king) violates all of nature.


## Masculinity & Gender

Both Macbeth and Lady Macbeth grapple with **gendered expectations**:

| Character | Gender Struggle |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Macbeth** | Told he's not a "man" if he shows mercy; masculinity tied to VIOLENCE |
| **Lady Macbeth** | Must "unsex" herself to access power; must strip away femininity |


> "When you durst do it, then you were a man."

This is a **toxic masculinity trap**: Macbeth is told his worth depends on his willingness to do violence. Lady Macbeth uses this to manipulate him, showing how **gender norms are weaponized** even by those they oppress.


## Blood — The Inescapable Stain

Blood is the play's **visceral symbol** — it appears more than 100 times:

| Type | Meaning | Scene |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Literal blood** | Violence, murder | Duncan's murder, Macduff's family |
| **Blood on hands** | Guilt | Lady Macbeth's sleep-walking |
| **Blood will have blood** | Cycle of vengeance | Macbeth's quote about revenge |
| **Thick blood** | Psychological horror | Macbeth sees a floating dagger with "gouts of blood" |


> "Will all great Neptune's ocean wash this blood / Clean from my hand?"

Macbeth asks if even the GOD'S ocean can cleanse him. The answer (the play shows us) is **NO**.

# Cross-Text Comparisons — Essential for Essay Questions

## Macbeth vs. Antigone
| Aspect | Macbeth | Antigone |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **Main conflict** | Personal vs. political law | Divine vs. civic law |
| **Response to law** | Violates it for power | Violates it for morality |
| **Fate** | Tries to control it | Accepts it |
| **Tragedy** | Self-inflicted | System-inflicted |


## Macbeth vs. Doctor Faustus
| Aspect | Macbeth | Faustus |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Source of downfall** | Ambition for power | Ambition for knowledge |
| **Catalyst** | Witches' prophecy | Pact with devil |
| **Awareness** | Gradual realization | Final绝望 |
| **Ending** | Acceptance with dignity | Begging for mercy |


## Macbeth vs. Mahabharata
| Aspect | Macbeth | Mahabharata |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Ambition** | Personal, tyrannical | Political, duty-bound |
| **Role of prophecy** | Manipulative | Dharma-guided |
| **Cycle of violence** | "Blood will have blood" | "Sattva, Rajas, Tamas" |
| **Resolution** | Restoration of monarchy | Aftermath of war |

# Dramatic Techniques Shakespeare Uses

| Technique | Example | Effect |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Soliloquy** | Macbeth's "dagger" and "brief candle" | Reveals inner conflict directly to audience |
| **Aside** | Macbeth's "stars, hide your fires" | Private thoughts while others are present |
| **Dramatic Irony** | Audience knows about Duncan's murder before Lennox | Creates tension and foreshadowing |
| **Imagery** | Blood, darkness, sleep, animals | Creates atmosphere, reinforces themes |
| **Stage directions** | "Enter a bloody child" | Visual shock forces audience to confront horror |
