# Rise of the Public Sphere

The eighteenth century saw the emergence of a new arena for public debate — coffeehouses, periodicals, salons, and print culture — where private citizens could discuss politics, literature, and ideas outside the control of the state or the church.

## Key Characteristics

- Growth of newspapers, journals, and pamphlets
- Coffeehouses as spaces of rational-critical debate
- Literacy expansion among the middle class
- Literature as a public, not courtly, institution
- Rise of the reading public and literary market

## Connection to Literature

- [[Alexander Pope]] navigated the new literary marketplace
- [[Jonathan Swift]] wrote political pamphlets and [[Satire]]
- [[Oliver Goldsmith]] contributed to periodical culture (e.g., *The Bee*)
- The novel emerged as a [[Bourgeois Society|bourgeois]] literary form
