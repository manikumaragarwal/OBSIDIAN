# Bourgeois Society

The eighteenth century saw the emergence of a powerful middle class — merchants, professionals, and tradespeople — who challenged aristocratic dominance and reshaped English culture, politics, and literature.

## Key Features

- Economic power through [[Capital and Trade|commerce and trade]]
- Values of industry, thrift, moral respectability
- Demand for education, literacy, and self-improvement
- Tension between old aristocracy and new wealth
- Foundation of the modern [[Public Sphere]]

## In Literature

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] satirises both aristocratic and bourgeois vanity
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] mocks the pretensions of all social classes
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] depicts a middle-class family's economic vulnerability
- The novel form itself arose to serve bourgeois readers
