# Age of Reason

The Age of Reason broadly refers to the eighteenth century in European intellectual history, characterised by a faith in rational inquiry, empirical science, and scepticism toward superstition and uncritical tradition.

## Key Ideas

- Human reason as the primary authority for knowledge
- Challenge to religious dogma and inherited authority
- Confidence in progress through science and education
- Critique of political tyranny and social inequality
- Emergence of secular moral philosophy

## In Literature

- [[Alexander Pope]] champions rational moderation in [[The Rape of the Lock]]
- [[Jonathan Swift]] exposes the limits of reason in [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]]
- [[Oliver Goldsmith]] balances reason with sentiment in [[The Vicar of Wakefield]]
