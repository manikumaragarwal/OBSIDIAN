# Enlightenment Thought

The Enlightenment was an intellectual movement emphasising reason, individual liberty, scientific method, and scepticism of traditional authority. It shaped the political, philosophical, and literary landscape of [[18th Century English Literature]].

> [!NOTE] Intellectual Movements Across Traditions
> Each period has its intellectual framework that shapes literature:
> - **18th Century**: Reason and empirical observation replace tradition and revelation
> - **Renaissance**: [[Doctor Faustus - Christopher Marlowe]] reflects tension between medieval theology and humanist aspiration
> - **Ancient Greece**: [[Aristotle's Poetics]] codifies rational principles of drama still used today
> - **Elizabethan/Jacobean**: [[Macbeth - William Shakespeare]] explores Renaissance anxiety about order, fate, and agency

## Core Principles

- Primacy of empirical reason over revelation
- Natural rights and social contract theory (Locke, Rousseau)
- Religious tolerance and critique of dogma
- Faith in the perfectibility of human society
- Separation of church and state

## Impact on Literature

- Writers used [[Satire]] to expose irrationality
- [[Alexander Pope]] expressed Enlightenment ideals of order and harmony
- [[Jonathan Swift]] questioned whether humanity deserved the title "rational"
- The [[Public Sphere]] created new audiences for print culture
