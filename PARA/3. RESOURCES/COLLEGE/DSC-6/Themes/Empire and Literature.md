# Empire and Literature

Eighteenth-century English literature is inseparable from the expansion of the [[British Empire in the 18th Century|British Empire]]. Writers both participated in and critiqued imperial ideology.

## Key Connections

- Travel narratives and voyage fiction reflected imperial exploration
- Trade goods (tea, silk, porcelain) appear as literary symbols
- Encounters with "others" forced questions about European superiority
- [[Satire]] of empire challenged complacent nationalism

## In the Texts

- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — Gulliver's own colonial instincts are satirised; the Houyhnhnms expose European savagery
- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — luxury goods from imperial trade fill Belinda's world ("all Arabia breathes from yonder box")
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — domestic economic anxieties linked to wider mercantile instability
