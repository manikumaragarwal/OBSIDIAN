# Capital and Trade

The eighteenth century was an era of unprecedented commercial growth. Trade, finance, and consumer culture transformed English society and left deep marks on its literature.

## Key Developments

- Expansion of the East India Company and Atlantic trade
- Growth of banking, credit, and the stock market (South Sea Bubble, 1720)
- Consumer revolution — new goods, fashions, luxuries
- Mercantile capitalism intertwined with [[British Empire in the 18th Century|empire]]

## In the Texts

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — Belinda's toilette showcases global commodities: "all Arabia breathes"
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — trade and commercial greed critiqued in the Voyage to Laputa
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — the Primrose family ruined by financial loss, reflecting economic precarity
