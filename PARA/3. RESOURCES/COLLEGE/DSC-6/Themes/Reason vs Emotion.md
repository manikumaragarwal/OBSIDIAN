# Reason vs Emotion

A central tension in [[18th Century English Literature]]: the [[Enlightenment Thought|Enlightenment's]] faith in reason clashed with the growing literary and philosophical attention to feeling, sentiment, and passion.

## The Tension

- Rationalists argued truth lies in logic and empirical evidence
- Sentimentalists argued moral knowledge comes through feeling
- Most writers occupied a middle ground, deploying both modes

## In the Texts

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — Belinda's passions overwhelm reason over a trivial loss
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — the purely rational Houyhnhnms lack warmth; the emotional Yahoos lack dignity → [[Rationalism vs Absurdity]]
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — Dr Primrose's faith and feeling save him where reason alone cannot
