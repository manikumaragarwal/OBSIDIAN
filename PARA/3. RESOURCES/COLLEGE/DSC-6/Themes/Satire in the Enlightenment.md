# Satire in the Enlightenment

[[Satire]] was the dominant literary mode of the [[Age of Reason]], used by writers to hold society up to rational scrutiny and expose the gap between ideals and behaviour.

## Why Satire Dominated

- The [[Enlightenment Thought|Enlightenment]] valued critical reason — satire *enacts* that critique
- Print culture and the [[Public Sphere]] created a mass audience for witty commentary
- Classical models (Horace, Juvenal) were revered by Augustan poets
- Social upheaval created plentiful targets

## Examples

- [[Alexander Pope]] — Horatian wit in [[The Rape of the Lock]]
- [[Jonathan Swift]] — Juvenalian fury in [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]]
- [[Oliver Goldsmith]] — gentle ironic observation in [[The Vicar of Wakefield]]
