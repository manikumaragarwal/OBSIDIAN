# Aristocratic Culture

The culture of the English aristocracy and upper gentry in the eighteenth century — centred on leisure, wit, fashion, and social display — provided rich material for [[Satire|satirists]].

## Key Characteristics

- Hampton Court, salons, and coffeehouses as social stages
- Emphasis on appearance, reputation, and rank
- Card games, assemblies, and fashionable amusements
- Patronage of the arts and letters
- Luxury consumption fuelled by [[Capital and Trade|imperial trade]]

## In the Texts

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — a panoramic portrait of aristocratic vanity and ritual
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — Lilliputian court politics mirror the English court
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — Squire Thornhill as the predatory aristocrat
