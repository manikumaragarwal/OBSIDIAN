# Social Hierarchy in 18th Century England

English society in the eighteenth century was rigidly stratified — from aristocracy and gentry through the expanding [[Bourgeois Society|middle class]] to labourers and the poor. Literature both reflected and interrogated these divisions.

## Structure

- **Aristocracy and landed gentry** — political power, inherited wealth
- **Emerging bourgeoisie** — merchants, professionals, financiers
- **Clergy** — often dependent on aristocratic patronage
- **Urban poor and rural labourers** — largely voiceless in print culture

## In the Texts

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — satirises aristocratic triviality and self-importance
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — exposes how power structures rely on arbitrary distinctions (Big-Endians vs Little-Endians)
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — the Primrose family's vulnerability to Squire Thornhill illustrates class exploitation
