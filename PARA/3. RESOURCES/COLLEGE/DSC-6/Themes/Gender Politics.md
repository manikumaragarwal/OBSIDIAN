# Gender Politics

Eighteenth-century literature reveals the rigid gender hierarchies of the period, while occasionally questioning them through [[Satire]] and [[Irony]].

## Key Issues

- Women defined primarily by beauty, marriage, and reputation
- Female education debated but largely ornamental
- "Honour" for women equated with sexual purity
- Commercial marriage market as economic transaction
- Emerging proto-feminist voices (Mary Astell, Mary Wollstonecraft later)

## In the Texts

- [[The Rape of the Lock]] — [[Belinda (Rape of the Lock)|Belinda's]] identity revolves around her appearance; the lock of hair symbolises female honour
- [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]] — female Yahoos and the Houyhnhnm mares reflect Swift's ambivalent gender attitudes
- [[The Vicar of Wakefield]] — Olivia's seduction and ruin enact the sexual double standard
