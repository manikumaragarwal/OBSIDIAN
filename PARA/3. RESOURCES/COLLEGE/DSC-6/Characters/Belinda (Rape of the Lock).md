# Belinda (Rape of the Lock)

Belinda is the heroine of [[Alexander Pope|Pope's]] [[The Rape of the Lock]], based on the real Arabella Fermor. She represents the brilliance and the vanity of [[Aristocratic Culture|aristocratic femininity]].

## Character

- Beautiful, witty, and at the centre of fashionable society
- Her morning "toilette" is staged as a religious ritual
- Her rage at the loss of the lock drives the poem's comic action
- Both admired and satirised by Pope

## Significance

- Embodies the [[PARA/3. RESOURCES/COLLEGE/DSC-6/Themes/Gender Politics|gender contradictions]] of her era: power through beauty, yet powerless in substance
- Her "honour" is equated with a lock of hair — trivialising and exposing the standards applied to women
- The sylphs who guard her are allegories of social reputation

## Key Quotation

> "If to her share some female errors fall, / Look on her face, and you'll forget 'em all." (II.17–18)
