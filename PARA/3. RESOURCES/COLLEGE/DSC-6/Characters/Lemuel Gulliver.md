# Lemuel Gulliver

Lemuel Gulliver is the narrator and protagonist of [[Jonathan Swift|Swift's]] [[Gulliver's Travels - Jonathan Swift]]. He is a surgeon and ship's captain whose four voyages expose the absurdities of European civilisation.

## Character

- Presented as a plain, credulous, middle-class Englishman
- Progressively disillusioned with humanity across the four voyages
- By Part IV, prefers the company of horses to humans
- His final misanthropy may itself be satirised by Swift

## Significance

- Gulliver's naïveté creates structural [[Irony]] — the reader sees what Gulliver cannot
- He is *both* a satirical weapon and a satirical target
- His transformation traces Swift's argument about [[Human Nature in Swift|human nature]]
- His final rejection of the Yahoos (i.e., humanity) raises the key question: is Swift endorsing or satirising this position?
