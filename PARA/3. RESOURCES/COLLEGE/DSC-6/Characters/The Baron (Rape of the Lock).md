# The Baron (Rape of the Lock)

The Baron is the male antagonist of [[The Rape of the Lock]], based on Lord Petre. He snips [[Belinda (Rape of the Lock)|Belinda's]] lock of hair, triggering the poem's mock-epic crisis.

## Character

- Driven by desire and social daring
- Builds an altar of love-tokens and prays for success
- Uses a pair of scissors (rendered in epic terms) to cut the lock
- Represents male aggression trivialised through [[Mock Epic|mock-epic]] framing

## Significance

- His act mirrors epic violations (rape of Helen, capture of Troy) but is absurdly petty
- The [[Irony]] lies in treating hair-cutting as a heroic deed
- Reflects the real social violation: unauthorised intimacy across gender boundaries → [[PARA/3. RESOURCES/COLLEGE/DSC-6/Themes/Gender Politics]]
