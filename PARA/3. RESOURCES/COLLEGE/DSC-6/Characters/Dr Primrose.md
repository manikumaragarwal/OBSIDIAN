# Dr Primrose

Dr Charles Primrose is the narrator and protagonist of [[Oliver Goldsmith|Goldsmith's]] [[The Vicar of Wakefield]]. He is a country vicar who endures a series of calamities with Christian patience.

## Character

- Gentle, pious, and well-meaning but somewhat vain and naïve
- Proud of his theological arguments (especially against remarriage)
- His unworldliness makes him vulnerable to exploitation
- Ultimately vindicated in his faith and moral resilience

## Significance

- Embodies the [[Sentimental Novel|sentimental]] ideal of domestic virtue → [[Family Morality]]
- His self-regard creates gentle [[Irony]] — Goldsmith both admires and gently mocks him
- Represents the virtues and vulnerabilities of the rural clergy → [[Religion and Morality]]
- His stoicism under suffering recalls Job — a Christian archetype

## Key Quotation

> "I was ever of opinion, that the honest man who married and brought up a large family, did more service than he who continued single, and only talked of population."
