# The Rape of the Lock

*The Rape of the Lock* (1712, expanded 1714) is a [[Mock Epic]] poem by [[Alexander Pope]]. It satirises a real incident in which Lord Petre (the [[The Baron (Rape of the Lock)|Baron]]) cut a lock of hair from Arabella Fermor ([[Belinda (Rape of the Lock)|Belinda]]).

## Summary

Pope elevates a trivial social quarrel to epic proportions, deploying the full machinery of classical epic — invocations, divine councils, armings, battles — to describe a fashionable lady's morning routine and the snipping of a curl. The poem is at once a gentle rebuke and a dazzling portrait of [[Aristocratic Culture|aristocratic London]].

## Cantos

1. Belinda's dream and morning toilette; [[Ariel (Rape of the Lock)|Ariel]] warns her
2. The boat journey on the Thames; Hampton Court
3. The card game (Ombre); the Baron cuts the lock
4. The Cave of Spleen; Umbriel's mischief
5. The battle of the beaux and belles; the lock ascends to the heavens

## Literary Devices

- [[Mock Epic]] — epic conventions applied to trivial matter
- [[Heroic Couplet]] — iambic pentameter couplets throughout
- [[Zeugma]] — "stain her honour, or her new brocade"

- [[Allegory]] — the sylphs as guardians of female reputation
