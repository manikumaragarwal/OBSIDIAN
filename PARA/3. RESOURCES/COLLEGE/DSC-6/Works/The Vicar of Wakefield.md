# The Vicar of Wakefield

** (1766) is a novel by [[Oliver Goldsmith]], blending [[Sentimental Novel|sentimental realism]] with gentle [[Satire]] and moral reflection.

## Plot Overview

Dr Charles Primrose ([[Dr Primrose]]), a country vicar, loses his fortune and endures a cascade of misfortunes — the seduction of a daughter, imprisonment for debt, fire, and family separation. Through patience and Christian virtue he is eventually restored to happiness.

## Themes

- [[Family Morality]] — loyalty, duty, and parental love
- [[Religion and Morality]] — Christian fortitude under suffering
- [[Social Hierarchy in 18th Century England]] — vulnerability of the clergy and rural poor
- [[PARA/3. RESOURCES/COLLEGE/Concepts/Reason vs Emotion|Reason vs Emotion]] — balance of sentiment and stoic endurance
- [[Capital and Trade]] — economic instability and class exploitation
- [[Satire]] — ironic distance from the vicar's naïveté

## Literary Devices

- [[Irony]] — gap between Dr Primrose's self-image and reality
- [[Sentimental Novel]] conventions — domestic virtue, pathos, moral resolution
- Picaresque elements — episodic structure, misadventures
- First-person unreliable narration

## Characters

- [[Dr Primrose]] — protagonist and narrator
- Olivia Primrose — seduced daughter
- Sophia Primrose — modest heroine
- Squire Thornhill — aristocratic villain
- Sir William Thornhill — benevolent disguised benefactor
- Mr Burchell — the Thornhill alter ego

## Historical Context

- Published in the decade before the American Revolution
- Reflects anxieties about [[Bourgeois Society|middle-class]] economic precarity
- Goldsmith's own experience of poverty and dispossession
- Engages with debates on [[Religion in 18th Century England]]
