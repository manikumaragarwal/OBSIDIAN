# 1. Gulliver's Travels as a Satire

**(Very Important – Appears repeatedly)**

## Introduction

- Define satire.
    
- Jonathan Swift uses travel narrative as a vehicle for satire.
    
- The novel attacks politics, religion, science, colonialism, and human pride.
    

## Body Points

### Satire on Politics

Evidence:

- Big-Endians vs Little-Endians.
    
- High-Heels vs Low-Heels.
    

Analysis:

- Mocks meaningless political and religious conflicts in England.
    

### Satire on Human Pride

Evidence:

- Gulliver feels giant among Lilliputians.
    
- Becomes insignificant in Brobdingnag.
    

Analysis:

- Human greatness depends on perspective.
    

### Satire on Science

Evidence:

- Academy of Lagado.
    

Analysis:

- Criticism of impractical scientific experiments.
    

### Satire on Human Nature

Evidence:

- Yahoos.
    

Analysis:

- Humans are greedy, violent, and irrational.
    

## Conclusion

- Swift uses fantasy to expose real social and moral problems.
    
- The novel remains one of the greatest satires in English literature.

# 2. Discuss Gulliver's Travels as a Political Satire

## Introduction

- Swift lived during intense political conflict.
    
- Many episodes indirectly criticize British politics.
    

## Body

### Lilliputian Court

Evidence:

- Rope dancing.
    

Analysis:

- Political promotion depends on pleasing rulers, not merit.
    

### High Heels and Low Heels

Evidence:

- Rival political groups.
    

Analysis:

- Satire on Whigs and Tories.
    

### Egg Controversy

Evidence:

- Big-Endians and Little-Endians.
    

Analysis:

- Religious disputes are often trivial.
    

### War with Blefuscu

Evidence:

- Absurd cause of conflict.
    

Analysis:

- Criticism of European wars.
    

## Conclusion

- Swift reveals the selfishness and absurdity of political institutions.

# 3. Gulliver's Travels as a Critique of Human Nature

**(Extremely Important)**

## Introduction

- Swift questions the belief that humans are naturally rational.
    

## Body

### Human Greed

Evidence:

- Yahoos fighting for shiny stones.
    

Analysis:

- Symbol of materialism.
    

### Human Violence

Evidence:

- Yahoo behaviour.
    

Analysis:

- Humans often act on instinct rather than reason.
    

### Human Pride

Evidence:

- Gulliver's changing self-image.
    

Analysis:

- Pride is based on illusion.
    

### Contrast with Houyhnhnms

Evidence:

- Rational horse society.
    

Analysis:

- Exposes flaws of humanity.
    

## Conclusion

- Swift presents a pessimistic but powerful view of mankind.

# 4. Compare the Houyhnhnms and the Yahoos

## Introduction

- They represent opposite aspects of existence.
    

## Body

### Houyhnhnms

- Rational
    
- Honest
    
- Self-controlled
    
- Live harmoniously
    

Evidence:

- No lies or corruption.
    

### Yahoos

- Greedy
    
- Violent
    
- Irrational
    
- Obsessed with possessions
    

Evidence:

- Fighting over stones.
    

### Swift's Purpose

- Create a contrast to evaluate humanity.
    

### Limitation

- Houyhnhnm society lacks emotion and individuality.
    

## Conclusion

- Swift suggests humans contain elements of both.

# 5. Significance of Lilliput

**(Frequently Asked)**

## Introduction

- First voyage.
    
- Establishes Swift's satirical method.
    

## Body

### Political Satire

- Rope dancing.
    
- Party divisions.
    

### Religious Satire

- Egg-breaking controversy.
    

### Human Vanity

- Tiny people behaving as if they are powerful.
    

### Relative Nature of Power

- Gulliver appears gigantic.
    

## Conclusion

- Lilliput exposes the pettiness of political and social institutions.

# 6. Significance of Brobdingnag

## Introduction

- Second voyage.
    
- Reverses Gulliver's position.
    

## Body

### Perspective on Humanity

- Gulliver becomes tiny.
    

### Exposure of Physical Imperfections

Evidence:

- Detailed descriptions of giant bodies.
    

Analysis:

- Human beauty is an illusion.
    

### King's Judgment of Europe

Evidence:

- King condemns European politics.
    

Analysis:

- Swift criticizes modern civilization.
    

## Conclusion

- Brobdingnag provides a moral critique of Europe.

# 7. The Academy of Lagado and Swift's Criticism of Science

## Introduction

- Part of Book III.
    

## Body

### Absurd Experiments

Evidence:

- Extracting sunbeams from cucumbers.
    
- Building houses from roof downward.
    

### Criticism

- Knowledge without practical value.
    

### Attack on Intellectual Vanity

- Scholars detached from reality.
    

### Historical Context

- Swift reacts to excessive faith in scientific progress.
    

## Conclusion

- Swift supports useful knowledge, not blind worship of science.

# 8. The Theme of Reason in Gulliver's Travels

## Introduction

- Reason is central to the novel.
    

## Body

### Houyhnhnms as Ideal Reason

Evidence:

- Rational government.
    

### Human Failure

Evidence:

- Yahoos.
    

### Gulliver's Transformation

Evidence:

- Begins admiring Houyhnhnms excessively.
    

### Swift's Warning

Analysis:

- Pure reason alone is not enough.
    

## Conclusion

- Swift advocates balanced reason rather than extreme rationalism.

# 9. Character of Gulliver

**(Almost always important)**

## Introduction

- Narrator and protagonist.
    
- Changes significantly through the voyages.
    

## Body

### Curious Traveller

Evidence:

- Desire for exploration.
    

### Observant Narrator

Evidence:

- Detailed descriptions.
    

### Adaptability

Evidence:

- Adjusts to different societies.
    

### Increasing Disillusionment

Evidence:

- Encounters with Yahoos.
    

### Final Alienation

Evidence:

- Prefers horses to humans.
    

Analysis:

- Shows psychological transformation.
    

## Conclusion

- Gulliver's changing perspective drives the novel's meaning.

# 10. The Theme of Relativity and Perspective

## Introduction

- Nothing is absolutely great or small.
    

## Body

### Lilliput

- Gulliver appears giant.
    

### Brobdingnag

- Gulliver appears tiny.
    

### Political Perspective

- Great events may be trivial.
    

### Moral Perspective

- Human pride becomes questionable.
    

## Conclusion

- Swift teaches humility through changing perspectives.

# 11. Is Gulliver's Travels a Utopian or Dystopian Text?

## Introduction

- The novel presents several imaginary societies.
    

## Body

### Utopian Features

- Order among Houyhnhnms.
    
- Rational government.
    

### Dystopian Features

- Lack of emotion.
    
- Suppression of individuality.
    

### Swift's Purpose

- No society is perfect.
    

## Conclusion

- The text questions all claims to perfection.

# Quotations Worth Memorizing

You don't need many.

### For Satire

> "Satire is a sort of glass wherein beholders do generally discover everybody's face but their own."

### For Human Nature

> "Man is a creature capable of reason."

(Used ironically throughout the novel.)

### For Yahoos

> "The most pernicious race of little odious vermin."

### For Political Satire

Use examples rather than quotations:

- Rope dancing
    
- High heels vs low heels
    
- Big-Endians vs Little-Endians
    

These examples often score better than memorized quotations in DU exams because they show textual knowledge and analysis.

### Exam Strategy

For a **20-mark answer**, follow:

**Introduction (1 paragraph)**

- Define theme/question.
    
- Mention Swift's purpose.
    

**Body (5–6 headings)**

- Point
    
- Evidence from text
    
- Analysis (most important part)
    

**Conclusion (1 paragraph)**

- Return to Swift's larger criticism of society and human nature.
    

That structure alone can comfortably fill 4–5 pages in a DU exam while staying analytical instead of merely retelling the story.
