*Gulliver's Travels* (1726), by [[Jonathan Swift]], is a prose [[Satire]] disguised as a travel narrative. Through four voyages, Swift attacks politics, science, imperialism, and the very idea of human rationality.

## Summary of the Four Voyages

### Part I — Lilliput
Gulliver is shipwrecked among six-inch-tall people whose petty political rivalries (Big-Endians vs Little-Endians) mirror English party politics. → [[Political Satire]]

### Part II — Brobdingnag
Among giants, Gulliver becomes the miniature curiosity. The Brobdingnagian king calls Europeans "the most pernicious race of little odious vermin." → [[Colonial Critique]]

### Part III — Laputa, Balnibarbi, etc.
Flying island of Laputa satirises impractical science and the Royal Society. Balnibarbi shows the ruin caused by theoretical schemes. → [[Rationalism vs Absurdity]]

### Part IV — Houyhnhnms
Rational horses govern a society where savage Yahoos (resembling humans) are the beasts. Gulliver prefers horses to humanity. → [[Human Nature in Swift]]


## Ideas 
1. What was the **initial reactions** of Lilliputians on seeing Gulliver's ? 
	- They try to control Gulliver by tying him down..
	- represents how society deals with unknown...   *Control first, Understand later*.
> 	Humans respond to the unknown not just with curiosity, but with control and fear disguised as order.
2. What was the **fear** Lilliputians first had when they discovered Gulliver? 
	- their superiority and sense of control was threatened. 
3. When Lilliputians bring massive amount of food for Gulliver, what does it represent? 
	- it talks about *relative perception*, what might be Huge for one, might be insignificant for others.
	- how riches have sooo much money, that poor people can't even fathom..
