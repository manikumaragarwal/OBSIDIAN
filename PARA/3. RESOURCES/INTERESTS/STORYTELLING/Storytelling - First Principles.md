# Storytelling — First Principles

> *People don't remember events. They remember transformation.*

## The Three Layers
```text
  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 3. THE PLOT (What Happens)                        │
│    The sequence of external events and actions.   │ ├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. THE EMOTION (What is Felt)                     │
│    The visceral reactions to the events.          │  ├───────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. THE IDENTITY (Who They Become)                 │
│    The deep, irreversible transformation.         │ └───────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## The Golden Rule
Every story fundamentally asks and answers one core question: **Who was this person before the inciting incident, and who are they now?** If the character could make the exact same choice at the end that they made at the beginning, the story has failed.

### Core Mechanics
- [[the 5-second moment is the irreversible change that defines a character]] — the exact decision/realization that permanently alters identity.
- [[stakes make the audience ask why should I care]] — the cost of failure that gives the narrative its weight.
- [[curiosity gaps delay the answer to sustain attention]] — exploiting the drive to resolve incomplete information.
- [[misdirection changes meaning not information]] — making the audience look the wrong way by confirming a false assumption.
- [[dramatic irony gives the audience dangerous knowledge]] — when we know a threat the character doesn't, creating suspense.
- [[the hourglass technique hides significance not information]] — revealing facts early but hiding their true meaning until later.
- [[contrast measures transformation]] — showing change by sharply juxtaposing the beginning against the end.
- [[the backpack technique gives the audience a goal to carry]] — handing the viewer the burden of an objective.
- [[the crystal ball plants predictions to create expectation]] — telling the audience what might happen to frame their focus.
- [[foreshadowing is a promise the story must keep]] — planting the seeds of the inevitable ending in plain sight.
- [[Chekhov's Gun demands that every planted detail fires]] — removing false promises and ensuring all components have narrative utility.
- [[open loops exploit the Zeigarnik effect to hold attention]] — leveraging cognitive tension by starting but not finishing thoughts.
- [[escalation requires every scene to increase stakes mystery or emotion]] — a story that flatlines loses the brain's attention.
- [[show don't tell lets the audience infer rather than be told]] — making the audience do the work deeply embeds the realization.
- [[vulnerability makes endings true not clever]] — emotional resonance comes from exposure, not outsmarting the viewer.
- [[character arc is measured by what the character is willing to do at the end that they weren't at the beginning]] — proof of transformation through action.
- [[information asymmetry between audience and character creates tension]] — manipulating who knows what to stretch engagement.
- [[in medias res drops the audience into motion]] — bypassing friction by starting at the point of highest momentum.
- [[dialogue reveals character through what is not said]] — conversation as a battlefield of subtext and hidden desires.
- [[emotional beats control when the audience feels]] — sequencing tension and release neurologically.
- [[conflict is the engine that makes character arc visible]] — friction is the only way a character's true nature is exposed.
- [[tension is unresolved conflict held over time]] — stretching the rubber band of anticipation before it snaps.

## Mental Checklist Before Publishing
1. **Is the transformation clear?** Can I name the Before and After states?
2. **Are the stakes identity-driven?** Does this threaten who they are, not just what they have?
3. **Is there a curiosity gap?** Have I opened a question early enough that they must stay to find out?
4. **Is the emotion earned?** Have I shown the vulnerability or just told them it exists?
5. **Are there dead spaces?** Does every scene escalate the tension, emotion, or mystery?
