# conflict is the engine that makes character arc visible
> *Character is revealed under pressure, not described in comfort. Without conflict, there is no change.*

## What It Is
Conflict is the collision of opposing forces. It is the fundamental engine of story. Without conflict, there is no pressure, and without pressure, a character has no reason to change. What a person does when facing real, costly conflict is who they truly are.

## Why It Works (The Psychology)
Humans are biologically wired to conserve energy and maintain equilibrium. We do not change unless we are forced to. Psychologically, conflict introduces cognitive dissonance and physical/emotional stakes that force a decision. A character's true values are only exposed when they are forced to choose between two things they care about. Conflict creates the necessary friction for growth.

## How It Works in Practice
Conflict operates on multiple levels simultaneously:
- **External conflict:** Character vs. character, character vs. nature, character vs. society.
- **Internal conflict:** Character vs. themselves (their desires, fears, flaws, and limiting beliefs).
- **Relational conflict:** Between characters who want fundamentally incompatible things.
- **Philosophical conflict:** Between two values or worldviews that cannot coexist.

The most powerful conflicts involve irreconcilable values within the *same* person. Furthermore, conflict drives the **Want vs. Need** dynamic. Characters pursue what they *want*; the story uses conflict to give them what they *need*. The conflict is often the friction between the two. 

*Note: Conflict is the event; tension is the sustained state of unresolved conflict.*

## Movie Examples
- **Whiplash:** Andrew wants greatness *and* humanity. He cannot have both. That irreconcilable internal conflict is the real engine of the film. He *wants* Fletcher's approval, but he *needs* to discover his own standard.
- **Parasite:** A class conflict so structurally deep and unforgiving that it has no possible resolution except violence. The external conflict perfectly mirrors the philosophical conflict.
- **Fight Club:** An internal conflict between the comfort of modern consumerism and raw, masculine freedom, externalized brilliantly as Tyler Durden.
- **Incendies:** The conflict is with the truth of the past—a truth the characters don't even know they're in conflict with until it crushes them.

## Connections to Theme
The conflict *is* the question the story is asking the audience. The resolution of that conflict (or the deliberate lack thereof) *is* the story's answer.

## Common Beginner Mistakes
- **Only External Conflict:** Having massive explosions and fistfights, but zero internal dimension or moral dilemmas for the protagonist.
- **Costless Resolution:** Conflict that resolves too easily, without forcing the character to sacrifice anything or make a difficult choice.

## Exercises to Practice
- State the core conflict of a film you love in exactly ONE sentence. Now, divide that sentence into its internal and external components.
