# NOTES
To outwork the competition, you should focus on **increasing the value of your output through creativity** rather than simply increasing the number of hours worked. Anthony Vicino argues that true success is found by leveraging **boredom** to activate the brain's creative networks, allowing you to produce higher-quality work in the same amount of time.

### The Science of Boredom: Two Neural Networks

The human brain operates through two primary systems that dictate how we work and create:

- **Executive Function Network (EFN):** This handles cognitive processes like planning, monitoring, and executing goals, including problem-solving and attentional control.
- **Default Mode Network (DMN):** This is active when our attention is not directed by external stimuli. It relies on internal memory and thoughts, making it the birthplace of **true creativity** and novel associations.

### Why Boredom Enhances Productivity

Boredom acts as a "preconditioning" tool for the mind. A 2014 study found that participants who performed highly boring tasks (like reading phone numbers out loud) significantly outperformed others in creative fluency tasks, such as finding novel uses for a brick.

- **Evolutionary Advantage:** Boredom evolved to motivate humans to experiment with new things and engage in **daydreaming**, a state of "decoupled processing" where internal thoughts are prioritised over external perception.
- **Value vs. Volume:** Because everyone has the same 24 hours, you cannot "outwork" others by just adding hours; you must produce **more value** per hour through creative insight.

### Why We Avoid Boredom
> See also [[Building your Personal Brand - AI NOTES|AI Brand Notes]]: "Deliver so much free value that people feel almost guilty not buying from you" — authenticity is the free value.

Despite its benefits, most people find boredom unpleasant, described as a state of feeling simultaneously stressed and lethargic.

- **The Pain of Silence:** In one study, 67% of men and 25% of women chose to **electrically shock themselves** rather than sit quietly alone for 15 minutes.
- **Existential Avoidance:** We often use distractions to avoid "existential questions" and the cognitive dissonance between who we are and who we want to be.
	> This connects directly to [[Am I a fake person]] — the avoidance of the question "who am I really?" through distraction.
- **Dopamine Seeking:** We have become "dopamine-seeking missiles," reaching for phones at the slightest hint of boredom to avoid mental reflection.

### Five Tactics to "Dumb Down" Your Smartphone

To make space for boredom, you must reduce your phone's power as a distraction tool.

| Tactic                       | Action                                                             | Benefit                                                                |
| :--------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Grayscale Mode**           | Turn the screen permanently to black and white.                    | Makes the phone less visually enticing and beautiful to look at.       |
| **Kill Notifications**       | Disable **all** alerts, including emails, texts, and social media. | Stops "attention terrorists" from dictating your mental agenda.        |
| **Tool vs. Toy**             | Remove games and entertainment apps.                               | Replaces mindless consumption with intentional tool usage.             |
| **The "Social Media Phone"** | Move social apps to a separate, cheaper phone kept on Wi-Fi only.  | Forces you to use social media with intention rather than as a reflex. |
| **Minimalist Home Screen**   | Keep a blank home screen or just one folder of essential apps.     | Acts as a "pattern interrupt" when you unlock your phone out of habit. |

### The Boredom Gym: Three Practical Exercises

Vicino suggests intentionally building your "boredom muscle" through these specific habits:

1. **Silent Commutes:** Stop listening to music, audiobooks, or podcasts while driving or walking to allow for silence.
2. **"Boring" Pomodoro Breaks:** During 5-minute work breaks, avoid the phone or social media. Instead, do something "boring" like staring out a window or doing air squats to keep the subconscious mind working on your primary task.
3. **Boredom "Meditation":** Sit still for 15 minutes with your eyes shut. Unlike traditional meditation, the goal is not to clear the mind but simply to **sit with the boredom** and let the mind wander wherever it wants without seeking distraction.

By giving yourself **permission to be bored**, you stop chasing easy dopamine and start creating the mental space required for high-value work.
