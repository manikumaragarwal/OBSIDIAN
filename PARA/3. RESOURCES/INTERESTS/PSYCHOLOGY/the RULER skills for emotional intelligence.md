# the RULER skills for emotional intelligence

## What It Is
The five core skills that comprise emotional intelligence, developed at Yale's Center for Emotional Intelligence.

## The Five Skills

### 1. Recognizing
Identifying emotions in oneself and others through:
- **Self**: Physiological sensations, thoughts, body states
- **Others**: Facial expressions, body language, posture, vocal intonation

Example: Can you tell how someone is feeling by their tone on the phone?

### 2. Understanding
Knowing where emotions come from and how they influence:
- Thinking and cognition
- Judgments and evaluations
- Decision-making processes
- Behavior and actions

Example: Why does anger arise? What's the theme around anger vs. sadness vs. pride?

### 3. Labeling
Building sophisticated vocabulary to describe emotional states precisely:
- Moving beyond basic terms (happy, sad, angry)
- Using nuanced language for complex feelings
- Understanding subtle differences between similar emotions

Example: The difference between jealousy and envy

### 4. Expressing
Knowing how and when to show emotions appropriately:
- Context-dependent (work vs. home vs. social)
- Culture-dependent (New York vs. Connecticut, American vs. Korean)
- Relationship-dependent (family vs. colleagues vs. strangers)

Example: Cultural rules about eye contact in different countries

### 5. Regulating
Developing strategies to manage emotions effectively:
- **Downregulating**: Reducing unwanted emotions (anger management, stress management)
- **Upregulating**: Initiating needed emotions (optimism induction, inspiration)
- **Maintaining**: Preserving positive states (flow, contentment)

Example: Taking a breath to activate parasympathetic nervous system

## Why "RULER"
The acronym spells a word that suggests mastery and measurement of emotional life.
