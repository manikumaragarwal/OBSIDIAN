# the mood meter tool for emotional self-awareness

![[Pasted image 20260330002226.png]]
## What It Is
A practical tool developed at Yale for building emotional self-awareness, consisting of two dimensions plotted on a grid.

## The Two Dimensions
1. **Pleasantness** (horizontal axis): Internal psychological state (-5 to +5)
   - +5: Most amazing day of your life
   - -5: Worst possible state

2. **Energy** (vertical axis): Physical activation state (-5 to +5)
   - +5: Ready to jump out of your seat
   - -5: About to fall asleep

## The Four Quadrants

### Yellow (Pleasant + High Energy)
- Feelings: Joy, excitement, elation, ecstasy
- Best for: Creative writing, brainstorming, generating ideas
- Example: Loud music, group collaboration

### Green (Pleasant + Low Energy)
- Feelings: Contentment, calm, satisfaction
- Best for: Building consensus, gathering opinions
- Example: Relaxed discussions, reflection

### Blue (Unpleasant + Low Energy)
- Feelings: Sadness, boredom, disappointment
- Important for: Expressing empathy, processing grief
- Example: Being there for someone in distress

### Red (Unpleasant + High Energy)
- Feelings: Anger, anxiety, frustration, irritation
- Useful when: Converted to passion, motivation for change
- Example: Fighting injustice, standing up for what's right

## The Cultural Bias
There's a common belief that everyone should be happy all the time. The mood meter shows this is unrealistic:
- Death, breakups, disappointments are part of life
- All emotions are valid and important
- It's what you do with them that matters

## Application
- Plot yourself multiple times daily
- Notice patterns and triggers
- Develop strategies for each quadrant
- Accept that all emotions have value
