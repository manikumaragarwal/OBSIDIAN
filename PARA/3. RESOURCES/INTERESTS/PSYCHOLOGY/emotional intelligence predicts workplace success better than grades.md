# emotional intelligence predicts workplace success better than grades

## Core Finding
In the workplace, emotional intelligence is a stronger predictor of success than academic credentials.

## The Fortune 100 Study
A study with a major corporation:
- 100 managers took an EI assessment
- CEO, CFO, and COO rated each manager
- **Key question**: "If you had to leave this company tomorrow, would you do anything to take that person with you?"
- **Result**: This question had the strongest correlation with emotional intelligence

## The Coffee Shop Criteria
Mark Brackett's personal hiring method:
- In the first 30 seconds, he asks: "Would I love to have a cup of coffee with this person?"
- If yes, the person is likely:
  - Interesting
  - Creative
  - Good at asking questions
  - Emotionally intelligent

## Why Grades Don't Matter
At Yale, Brackett tells students:
- In four years, everyone will look the same on paper
- Great SAT scores, perfect GPA, studied abroad, plays obscure instrument
- What differentiates is emotional intelligence
- He doesn't look at grades when hiring
- What matters: Can they work in a team? Do they make you feel comfortable?

## The Implication
For hiring and career success:
- Technical skills are table stakes
- Emotional intelligence is the differentiator
- People want to be around emotionally intelligent individuals
- EI creates better teams, better leadership, better outcomes
