# cultural differences in emotional expression

## Core Idea
Emotional expression is deeply influenced by cultural context. What's appropriate in one culture may be rude or inappropriate in another.

## Examples from the Talk

### Eye Contact
- **American culture**: Direct eye contact shows engagement and honesty
- **Korean culture**: Direct eye contact can be considered rude
- Mark Brackett's experience: His American teacher warned him not to make eye contact in Korea, but his Korean teacher found his avoidance strange

### Physical Expression
- **Martial arts studio**: Bowing is expected and respectful
- **Academic presentation**: Audience won't bow to the speaker
- **New York City friends**: Openly express depression, bloating, tiredness
- **New Haven, Connecticut**: More reserved emotional communication

### Regional Differences
Even within the same country:
- New York City: More emotionally expressive
- Connecticut: More emotionally reserved
- Mark's joke: "I haven't gotten a hug in 10 years" (exaggerated, but illustrates the difference)

## The Implication
Emotional intelligence requires:
- Awareness of cultural rules and norms
- Flexibility in emotional expression
- Sensitivity to context
- Avoiding assumptions about what's "normal"
