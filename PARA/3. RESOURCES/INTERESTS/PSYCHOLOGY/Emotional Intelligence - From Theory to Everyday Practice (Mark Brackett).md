# Emotional Intelligence: From Theory to Everyday Practice

## Overview
This is a talk by **Mark Brackett**, Director of the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence, discussing the history, research, and practical applications of emotional intelligence over 20 years of work at Yale.

## Key Speakers & Origins
- **Peter Salovey** (Yale President) - originator of the theory of emotional intelligence
- **Jack Mayer** (Brackett's doctoral advisor) - co-developer of the theory
- **Daniel Goleman** - popularized emotional intelligence with his 1995 book
- **Mark Brackett** - current director of Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence

## Historical Context
- **1990**: Salovey and Mayer wrote seminal article on emotional intelligence (initially unnoticed)
- **1995**: Goleman's popular book brought the concept to mainstream awareness
- The idea went largely unknown for 5 years after the original paper
- Ancient philosophers and stoics viewed emotions as antithetical to reason
- Modern view: "Emotions matter and they matter a great deal"

## The Mood Meter
A tool for self-awareness with four quadrants based on two dimensions:
1. **Pleasantness** (internal psychological state): -5 to +5
2. **Energy** (physical state): -5 to +5

### Four Colors/Quadrants:
- **Yellow**: Pleasant + High Energy (joy, excitement, creativity)
- **Green**: Pleasant + Low Energy (contentment, calm)
- **Red**: Unpleasant + High Energy (anger, anxiety, frustration)
- **Blue**: Unpleasant + Low Energy (sadness, boredom)

### Applications:
- Yellow: Great for creative writing, brainstorming, generating ideas
- Green: Wonderful for building consensus, getting opinions together
- Blue: Important for expressing empathy
- Red: Useful when converted to passion; can motivate action against injustice

## Why Emotions Matter
Research shows emotions affect:
1. **Attention, Memory, and Learning**
   - Anxiety makes concentration difficult (rumination/obsessing)
   - Example: Bullied child can't focus on learning when brain is focused on emotional distress
   - Even positive anticipation (vacation excitement) can impair focus

2. **Decision-Making and Judgment**
   - Study on teachers: Mood state affected essay grading by 1-2 full grade differences
   - 90% of teachers denied their emotions influenced their grading (but data showed otherwise)
   - Negative moods → narrow thinking; positive moods → expansive thinking

3. **Relationship Quality**
   - We avoid people who display constant negative affect
   - We avoid people who constantly misread us

4. **Physical and Mental Health**
   - Can't be healthy without effective emotional strategies and self-awareness

## The RULER Skills
The five core skills of emotional intelligence:

1. **Recognizing** emotions in self and others
   - Facial expressions, body language, posture, vocal intonation
   - Self-awareness of physiological and cognitive states

2. **Understanding** where emotions come from
   - How emotions influence thinking, judgments, decisions, behavior
   - The underlying reasons for emotional states

3. **Labeling** emotions with precise vocabulary
   - Building sophisticated vocabulary to describe ourselves clearly
   - Example: Difference between jealousy and envy
   - Jealousy = about relationships/dynamics (can lead to violence/aggression)
   - Envy = wanting what someone else has (can inspire achievement)

4. **Expressing** emotions appropriately
   - Knowing the how and when based on context and culture
   - Cultural differences in emotional expression
   - Rules vary between contexts (New York vs Connecticut, American vs Korean culture)

5. **Regulating** emotions effectively
   - Not just downregulating negatives (anger management, stress management)
   - Also: optimism induction, happiness maintenance, flow state preservation
   - Strategies: positive self-talk, reappraisal, visualization, yoga, exercise

## Measurement of Emotional Intelligence
Three approaches:
1. **Self-report** - Limited by self-knowledge and reference points
2. **Informant/360 reports** - Often measure reputation rather than skills
3. **Ability-based assessment** - Solving emotion-related problems (preferred)
   - Original MSCEIT test by Salovey, Mayer, and Caruso
   - New virtual tests using avatars and gaming

## Research Findings

### On Students:
- Higher EI → less anxiety and depression
- Less likely to abuse alcohol, drugs, cigarettes
- Less aggressive, less likely to bully
- Perceived as better leaders
- More attentive, less hyperactive
- Better academic performance

### On Educators:
- More positive about teaching
- Receive more principal support
- Greater job satisfaction
- Less burnout

### On Workplace:
- Strongest correlation: "Would you do anything to take that person with you if you left the company?"
- The "Coffee Shop Criteria": Would you want to have coffee with this person?
- EI matters more than grades for hiring decisions

## The Meta-Moment Tool
A 6-step self-regulation process:

1. **Something happens** (trigger)
2. **Emotional hijack** - awareness of how emotions affect thinking/physiology/behavior
3. **Breathe** - activate parasympathetic nervous system, bring prefrontal cortex back online
4. **See your best self** - visualization of ideal response
5. **Strategize** - choose adaptive vs maladaptive strategies
6. **Problem solve** - empathize, take perspective, find solutions

Key insight: "If you take the six steps seriously, you can avoid the 12 steps later."

## Gareth's Story
A boy who was horrifically bullied in elementary school but transformed through emotional intelligence education:

- Was a failing student in 5th and 6th grade
- Entered a middle school that embraced EI
- Wrote a powerful poem about bullying
- Initially too scared to share publicly
- Teacher respected his boundary but shared with other teachers
- Poem received recognition → built self-esteem
- Eventually read it to class → standing ovation
- Now in high school, wants to be a poet
- Demonstrates: You can't show academic ability when feeling oppressed/unvalued
