# Schema Therapy: Core Beliefs and Early Maladaptive Schemas

## Overview
Schema therapy, developed by Jeffrey Young, focuses on identifying and changing deeply held patterns (schemas) formed in childhood that create chronic psychological problems. Schemas are broad, pervasive themes that develop when core emotional needs are not met.

## What Are Schemas?

### Definition
**Schemas** are:
- Deeply held patterns formed in childhood/adolescence
- Templates for interpreting experiences
- Self-perpetuating (confirm themselves)
- Difficult to change
- Activated by relevant life events
- Maladaptive in adulthood

### Core Emotional Needs (When Met = Healthy Development)
1. **Secure attachment** - safety, stability, nurturance
2. **Autonomy** - independence, competence
3. **Freedom of expression** - spontaneity, emotions, needs
4. **Playfulness** - spontaneity, fun, joy
5. **Realistic limits** - reasonable boundaries, discipline

### When Needs Are Not Met
Result: **Early Maladaptive Schemas (EMS)** - core beliefs about self, others, and world

## Schema Coping Styles

### How People Respond to Schema Activation

**1. Schema Surrender** 🏳️
- Give in to schema
- Accept it as truth
- Don't fight it
- Passively endure

**Your pattern:**
- "I'm fraud, that's just true"
- Accepting shame without challenge
- Feeling powerless to change

**2. Schema Avoidance** 🚫
- Avoid situations triggering schema
- Suppress or deny schema
- Overcompensate opposite way
- Behavioral avoidance

**Your pattern:**
- Avoiding truth-telling (avoidance of shame trigger)
- Avoiding scores, relatives
- Creating new identity to avoid old one
- Compulsive lying = avoidance strategy

**3. Schema Overcompensation** 💪➡️
- Fight schema by doing opposite
- Often exaggerated opposite
- Can be adaptive or maladaptive

**Your pattern:**
- Perfectionism to compensate for failure schema
- High achievement attempts to prove worth
- Trying to appear confident (while feeling fraudulent)
- Over-preparing, over-studying

### Your Pattern: Mostly Avoidance + Some Overcompensation
- Avoidance through lying and dissociation (main strategy)
- Overcompensation through perfectionism and achievement seeking
- Eventually: Need to move to schema healing

## Limited Reparenting (Therapy Technique)

### What It Is
Therapist provides corrective emotional experiences that were missing in childhood.

### How It Helps
- Meets unmet emotional needs
- Corrects defective schema messages
- Provides "good parent" model
- Helps internalize healthier voice

### Your Unmet Needs → Therapy Provides
| Childhood Need | Schema Result | Therapy Provides |
|----------------|---------------|------------------|
| Acceptance with imperfections | Defectiveness/Shame | Unconditional positive regard |
| Support through failures | Failure schema | Normalization, encouragement |
| Safe emotional expression | Emotional inhibition | Validation, empathy |
| Reasonable standards | Unrelenting standards | Compassion, flexibility |
| Healthy boundaries | Approval-seeking | Autonomy, self-worth teaching |

## Schema-Focused Cognitive Techniques

### 1. Schema Flashcards
- Write schema on card
- List evidence against it
- Carry and read when triggered

**Example for Defectiveness:**
"My worth is inherent, not based on performance" +
evidence: curiosity, resilience, programming interest, honesty in therapy

### 2. Historical Testing
- Examine schema origins
- Was schema adaptive in childhood? (Yes!)
- Is it still adaptive now? (No)
- Update it for current reality

### 3. Schema Diary
Track:
- What triggered schema?
- What were thoughts/feel?
- What was schema's message?
- How did I respond?
- What would be healthier response?

### 4. Continuum Work
- Rate schema from 0-100% rather than all-or-nothing
- "I'm 40% defective, 60% worthy"
- Develops nuance and flexibility

## Schema Mode Model

### Mode: Momentary State
Different "parts" or modes that take over based on schema activation:

### 1. Child Modes (Vulnerable)
**Vulnerable Child:**
- Feels: sad, scared, lonely, defective
- Holds: Core pain, unmet needs
- Your experience: 14-year-old trauma victim, scared you're still holding

**Angry Child:**
- Feels: angry, rageful, frustrated
- Responds to: Unfairness, unmet needs
- Your experience: Possibly anger at pressure, abuser, situation

**Impulsive Child:**
- Acts on impulses
- Can't delay gratification
- Your experience: Possibly compulsive lying pattern

### 2. Parent Modes (Healthy vs. Maladaptive)
**Healthy Adult:**
- Problem-solves
- Acts maturely
- Meets needs appropriately
- Your growth:
Need to strengthen this mode

**Punitive Parent:**
- Criticizes, punishes
- Harsh, demeaning
- Messages: "You're bad, defective, failed"
- Your experience: Strong internal critic
- Origin: Internalized expectations/trauma

**Demanding Parent:**
- Pushes pressure, demands
- Unrelenting standards
- Messages: "You must achieve more" "Not enough"
- Your experience: Very strong
- Origin: Academic pressure, family expectations

### 3. Coping Modes (Maladaptive)
**Compliant Surrenderer:**
- Gives up, submits
- Passive

**Detached Protector:**
- Withdraws, avoids
- Emotional numbing
- Dissociates
- Your experience: Primary pattern
- Function: Protects vulnerable child from pain

**Overcompensator:**
- Acts opposite of schema
- Dramatic, attention-seeking
- Perfectionistic
- Your experience: Achievement striving, trying to prove worth

### Your Current Mode Pattern

1. **Vulnerable Child** activated (failure, defectiveness schemas)

3. OR **Overcompensator** (perfectionism, achievement)
4. **Punitive Parent** criticizes (you're fraud/bad)

## Comparison: CBT vs. Schema Therapy

| CBT | Schema Therapy |
|-----|----------------|
| Focus on present thoughts | Focus on childhood origins |
| Symptom-focused | Core personality patterns |
| Short-term | Longer-term |
|
Addresses specific thoughts | Addresses whole schemas |
| Logical disputing | Experiential + cognitive work |
| Works well for: moderate problems | Works well for: chronic, complex issues, personality patterns |

- Recurrent depression/anxiety

- Treatment-resistant cases
- Personality disorders
- Chronic depression/anxiety
- Eating disorders
