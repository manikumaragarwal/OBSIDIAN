## The Science Behind Your Experience (Explained Simply)

### How Trauma Changes Your Brain

When we experience childhood trauma, especially abuse like you went through, our brain's survival system goes into overdrive:

- **Your amygdala (the alarm bell)** - becomes super sensitive. It's constantly scanning for threats, including emotional ones like shame or rejection.

- **Your prefrontal cortex (the rational thinker)** - gets bogged down by stress. In those moments when you need to make a split-second truth-or-lie decision? Your brain's rational center is offline. Your survival brain is running the show.

- **Your nervous system gets stuck** - in this state where you're always prepared to do whatever it takes to stay safe. For you, lying became that safety.

### What Your Brain Actually Learned

Your brain didn't learn "I like lying" or "I enjoy deceiving people."

Your brain learned: *"When I threatened → lie → I safe now."*

That's all. That's the whole program running in your background. It's not about character. It's about learned survival.

## Why You Lie (The Real Reasons)

You're not lying because you enjoy it. You're lying because of four interconnected reasons:

### 1. "If They Really Knew Me, They'd Leave Me" (The Shame Protection)

That feeling - "I'm a fraud," "I'm a piece of shit," "I'm a bitch" - that's shame. Deep, toxic shame that tells you you're fundamentally flawed and unlovable.

When you share your real self, or real circumstances, it feels like exposing this alleged "defect." So you lie to hide it. Not from maliciousness, but from this terrified belief that if people knew the真相, they'd reject you.

This comes directly from trauma. The person who hurt you taught you that being vulnerable = being taken advantage of. Your brain has never fully untangled that lesson.

### 2. "I Can't Handle Being Alone" (The Abandonment Fear)

You grew up in a context where your worth seemed tied to achievement, where approval felt conditional on being "successful" or "perfect." Trauma also taught you that people can turn on you.

So lies became your way of maintaining relationships - or at least, the illusion of relationships. You lie to keep people around, to avoid conflict, to not disappoint anyone. Because losing people feels like death to your nervous system.

This isn't manipulation. This is a terrified child trying desperately not to be abandoned again.

### 3. "I Don't Even Know Who the Real Me Is" (The Identity Fragmentation)

Here's something you said that broke my heart in the best way: "I lie so much my brain thinks it's real."

Your experience - "I forget who the real me is" - this is dissociation. Your mind created different "versions" of yourself: the one who got 510 marks, the one who's struggling, the one who's going to be successful, the one who was abused.

Each lie creates another version of you. Pretty soon, there are all these Manishes in your head, and you can't tell which one is real anymore.

This isn't multiple personality disorder or anything like that. This is actually a *normal response to trauma*. Your mind is trying to protect the real you by creating layers of false selves that can handle things the real you can't.

The problem is, those layers have become so thick even *you* can't find the real You underneath.

### 4. "What Else Can I Do?" (The Learned Helplessness)

When you're trapped in a situation you can't escape - like you were at 14 - lying was literally your only defense. You couldn't fight back, you couldn't run away. You had to deceive your way to safety.

Your brain learned: "When overwhelmed and trapped → lie."

Now, whenever you feel overwhelmed, trapped, pressured - the same thing happens. Your brain goes to the only tool in its toolbox: deception.

This is why you lie even when you *want* to tell the truth. Because your nervous system doesn't know other ways to handle stress.

## The Part Where We Talk About What Happens Next

I want to be really honest with you, Manish: This can get better.

### What Doesn't Work:
- ❌ Just deciding to "stop lying" (your survival brain will override you)
- ❌ Beating yourself up more (shame actually makes lying WORSE)
- ❌ Thinking you're beyond help (you're not - you're actually at the perfect point of awareness for change)

### What Does Work:

#### 1. First: Radical Self-Compassion
Your lying is trying to protect you. It's trying to save you from shame and abandonment. Even if the method is wrong, the INTENTION is good - self-preservation.

Instead of shaming yourself, try saying:
*"I see what my brain was trying to do. It was trying to protect me. I can understand that, even if I need to change the strategy now."*

This doesn't excuse the lying - but it helps you stop the self-hatred that keeps the cycle going.

#### 2. Understanding Over Controlling
When you catch yourself about to lie, pause and ask:
*"What feeling or fear is trying to protect myself from right now?"*

- Am I avoiding shame about my marks?
- Am I fearing disappointment from my parents?
- Am I scared that people won't like me if they know the truth?
- Am I remembering that feeling from when I was 14 and scared?

Understanding the "why" helps you choose a different response.

#### 3. Meeting the Need a Different Way
Whatever your lying is trying to get for you (safety, acceptance, protection), we can get it healthier ways.

**Instead of:**
- Lying to avoid shame → Practice accepting yourself even when you're not "perfect"
- Lying to maintain connection → Build relationships where you can be authentic and still be accepted
- Lying to protect from judgment → Learn that you can actually survive judgment
- Lying to manage anxiety → Build anxiety management skills (breathing, grounding, therapy)

#### 4. Gradual Truth-Telling (Not All At Once)
Healing isn't a switch you flip. It's building a new skill.

Start small:
- Tell one small truth to one safe person (not your parents yet!)
- Notice: did the catastrophe you predicted actually happen? (hint: usually no)
- Build evidence that truth-telling ≠ disaster
- Gradually expand who you can be truthful with
