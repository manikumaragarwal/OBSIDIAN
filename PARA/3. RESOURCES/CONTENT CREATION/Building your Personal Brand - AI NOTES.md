To build a personal brand effectively, you must move from a **forgetful brand**—posting randomly—to an **intentional brand**, where you own the associations people have with your content. Branding is defined as the **intentional pairing** of relevant things done consistently, while "brand" is the resulting byproduct: when an audience inherently associates those things with you.

Below is a hierarchical guide to building an intentional brand, based on the course provided.

### **I. The Brand Foundation: Strategy & Positioning**

- **The Brand Journey Framework (Reverse Engineering)**
    
    - Define your **desired outcome** first to ensure every decision aligns with your end goal.
    - Ask: What must I be known for, what must I do, and what must I learn to reach that outcome?
    - **Tip: Define "anti-associations."** Knowing what you _do not_ want to be associated with is often more important for protecting your reputation than knowing what you do want.
- **Market Positioning**
    
    - **Find the Gap:** Do not just blend in; identify what is missing in your industry and own that space.
    - **Contrarian Views:** Stand out by sharing a **core conviction** that differs from the market norm.
    - **Tip: Identify disagreement.** Ask yourself: "What are people in my space saying that I dishhlagree with?".
- **The Three-Part Brand Story Framework**
    
    - **1. The Catalyst:** Why your brand exists and what opportunity or gap you saw.
    - **2. The Core Truth:** What makes your company different or your unique convictions.
    - **3. The Proof:** Ongoing patterns of action and case studies that establish your credibility.
    - **Tip: Story over Traits.** Instead of calling yourself "hardworking," tell a specific story that demonstrates it.

### **II. Content Strategy: Creation & Distribution**

- **The 80/20 Rule for Topics**
    
    - Spend **80% of your time** on your core subject to build world-class expertise and authority.
    - Use the remaining **20% for "interest stacking"**—sharing personal hobbies (e.g., motorcycles, music) to humanise your brand and provide more "at bats" for the audience to connect with you.
    - **Tip: Start Narrow.** Build early trust by being great at one specific thing before expanding into adjacent topics.
- **Mediums and Platforms**
    
    - **Video as Leverage:** Video is the highest-leverage medium because it can be repurposed into audio, transcripts, and short-form clips.
    - **Platform Prioritisation:** Focus on **2-3 platforms** to avoid single-channel risk while maintaining an "**Eye of Sauron**" focus on one priority platform at a time.
    - **Tip: Avoid the "Perfect Post Trap."** Speed wins in the early stages; progress is more valuable than perfection.
- **The Accordion Method (Quality vs. Quantity)**
    
    - Post at a **high volume** initially to acquire data on what your audience deems as "quality".
    - Once you identify top-performing content (the top 10%), "compress the accordion" by putting more effort into fewer, higher-value pieces.
    - **Tip: Mine for Gold.** Use a **waterfall distribution model** to extract dozens of micro-content pieces (quotes, carousels, shorts) from one long-form video or newsletter.

### **III. Strategic Team Building: Scaling Execution**

- **Hiring for Bottlenecks**
    
    - Never hire for convenience; hire specifically to **remove constraints** in your process.
    - Prioritise **platform-specific specialists** (e.g., a "YouTube Editor" or "LinkedIn Writer") over general social media managers.
    - **Tip: Culture over Skills.** It is easier to train technical skills than it is to change a person’s hunger, empathy, or adaptability.
- **The 30-60-90 Day Onboarding Plan**
    
    - **30 Days:** Orientation, learning workflows, and small tasks to build momentum.
    - **60 Days:** Taking independent ownership of core responsibilities.
    - **90 Days:** Full integration with minimal supervision and a formal performance review.
- **The Maker vs. Manager Schedule**
    
    - Protect your team’s productivity by separating the day: **mornings for "Maker Time"** (uninterrupted flow work) and **afternoons for "Manager Time"** (meetings).
    - **Tip: Radical Transparency.** Own your mistakes as a leader and communicate pivots immediately to maintain team trust.

### **IV. Monetisation: Trust and Offer Stacks**

- **The Currency of Trust**
    
    - Monetisation should follow trust, not precede it.
    - **Formula:** Give away the **knowledge for free** and sell the **execution**.
    - **Tip: The "Guilt" Threshold.** Deliver so much free value that people feel almost guilty not buying from you.
- **The Offer Progression Stack**
    
    1. **Free Content:** Builds awareness.
    2. **Lead Magnets:** Captures contact data (checklists, playbooks).
    3. **Low Ticket ($10-$100):** Removes the barrier to entry (e-books).
    4. **Mid-Tier ($500-$5,000):** High-commitment buyers (courses, coaching).
    5. **High Ticket ($10,000+):** Direct consulting or "done-for-you" services.
