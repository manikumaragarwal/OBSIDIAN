# Identity Goal Prematurity: Why Sharing Your Intentions Triggers Fake Completion Dopamine

Sharing your goals before executing them is a form of "Identity Theft" against your future self. The act of telling someone about a major change triggers a premature dopamine hit of completion, which creates a wild gap between your stated intention and your actual behavior.

## Breakdown
- **The Identity Trap:** When you tell people "I am becoming a runner," your brain receives the social validation of *being* a runner without the effort of *running*.
- **The Dopamine Gap:** The social reward reduces the biological pressure to actually execute the goal because the "Identity Goal" has already been "completed" in the mind of others.
- **Silent Execution:** ==True change happens in the silence between intention and result==. Every time you share an un-executed goal, you are bleeding off the motivation needed to finish it.

## The Why
Validation is an addictive substance. By recognizing that social approval acts as a "Fake Finish Line," a creator can move toward "Proof of Work" models where the result is shared only after the execution phase is complete, ensuring the dopamine is reserved for the victory, not the promise.
